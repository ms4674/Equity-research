# Meta: Hyperscale & Neocloud Commitments — Timeline and Amounts

## Executive Summary

Meta Platforms (NASDAQ: META) has committed to an unprecedented $600 billion in U.S. AI data center infrastructure spending by 2028, supplemented by over $60 billion in third-party neocloud and cloud capacity agreements. This document provides a comprehensive timeline and quantification of Meta's hyperscale (owned/operated) and neocloud (third-party GPU cloud) commitments, including capital expenditures, lease obligations, financing arrangements, and third-party compute contracts.

## 2. Neocloud Commitments — Third-Party GPU Cloud Contracts

Meta has contracted with multiple neocloud and cloud providers to supplement its owned infrastructure, securing external GPU capacity for AI training and inference.

### 2.1 Summary of Neocloud and Third-Party Cloud Deals

### 2.3 Strategic Rationale for Neocloud Contracts

1. **Speed to capacity**: Neocloud providers can deliver GPU capacity 12–24 months faster than Meta's owned data center builds, bridging the gap during the construction ramp.
2. **GPU supply access**: Neoclouds like CoreWeave and Nebius have direct NVIDIA allocation relationships, providing Meta access to scarce Blackwell/Rubin GPUs that may be supply-constrained through 2027.
3. **Flexibility**: Third-party contracts provide optionality — Meta can scale up or down without the fixed-cost risk of owned infrastructure.
4. **Geographic diversification**: Nebius operates in Finland, Iceland, and other international locations, providing geographic compute diversity.
5. **Risk management**: Distributes execution risk across multiple providers rather than concentrating all infrastructure buildout internally.

## 7. Risk Factors

1. **Execution risk**: The $600B buildout plan requires simultaneous construction across multiple sites, competing for scarce construction labor, power transformers (128-week lead times), and electrical infrastructure.
2. **GPU supply concentration**: Dependence on NVIDIA for both owned and neocloud capacity creates single-vendor risk. NVIDIA supply constraints through 2027 could limit capacity delivery.
3. **Demand uncertainty**: If AI monetization (ads, subscriptions, enterprise) underperforms expectations, the fixed costs of committed infrastructure could pressure margins.
4. **Counterparty risk**: CoreWeave and Nebius are relatively young companies with significant leverage. CoreWeave carries ~$11B in debt; service disruptions or financial distress could affect capacity delivery.
5. **Regulatory risk**: Data center permitting, power grid approvals, and environmental reviews could delay projects. Power grid constraints are already extending data center timelines by 24–72 months in some regions.
6. **Capital allocation**: $600B in cumulative capex through 2028 competes with shareholder returns. Meta spent $26.3B on buybacks and $5.3B on dividends in 2025; the buyback program was paused in Q4 2025 to fund infrastructure.
