## 1. Top 5 Customers & Revenue Concentration

Arm does not disclose exact revenue percentages by individual customer in its public filings but identifies customer concentration as a material risk factor. Based on the 20-F filing disclosures, earnings commentary, and publicly known licensing/royalty relationships, the five most significant customers by estimated revenue contribution are:

| Rank | Customer | Relationship to Arm | Revenue Significance |
|------|----------|---------------------|----------------------|
| 1 | **Qualcomm** | Largest licensee by volume; Snapdragon SoCs are Arm-based across mobile, automotive, IoT, and PC. Major royalty contributor through billions of annual chip shipments. | Historically estimated at ~20%+ of total Arm revenue (royalty + licensing). Qualcomm has a long-standing architectural license. |
| 2 | **Apple** | Designs custom Arm-based silicon (A-series, M-series) for iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, and Vision Pro. Premium tier royalty payer. | Estimated ~10-15% of total revenue. Apple's high ASP devices and custom core designs generate significant per-unit royalties. |
| 3 | **Samsung Electronics** | Uses Arm cores in Exynos SoCs (smartphones, wearables) and is a major foundry for other Arm-based designs. Dual role as both licensee and royalty contributor. | Estimated ~8-12% of total revenue across direct Exynos royalties and licensing agreements. |
| 4 | **MediaTek** | Leading fabless chip designer using Arm cores (Dimensity series) for smartphones, smart TVs, IoT, and Chromebooks. High-volume licensee. | Estimated ~7-10% of total revenue. MediaTek ships very high volumes, particularly in mid-range and value segments. |
| 5 | **NVIDIA** | Uses Arm-based Grace CPUs for data center, Arm-based Bluefield DPUs, and Jetson platforms for edge AI/robotics. Recently launched Vera CPU (88 Arm cores). | Estimated ~5-8% of total revenue and growing rapidly. NVIDIA's data center CPU ramp is a major licensing and royalty growth driver. |

**Other notable customers:** AWS (Graviton custom silicon), Alphabet/Google (Axion processors), Microsoft (Cobalt custom silicon), AMD, Intel, Broadcom, and Marvell.

**Key concentration risk:** Arm's 20-F filing explicitly identifies "dependence on a limited number of customers for a significant portion of its revenue" as a principal risk. The top 5 customers collectively represent an estimated 50-65% of total revenue.

## 4. Revenue Segment Deep-Dive

### 4a. Royalty Revenue -- Drivers & Unit Economics

Royalty revenue is earned on a per-chip basis when licensees ship products containing Arm IP. The key structural drivers expanding royalty revenue per chip:

| Driver | Impact | Status |
|--------|--------|--------|
| **Armv9 adoption** | ~2x royalty rate vs. Armv8 per chip | >30% of royalty revenue in Q4 FY25, up from ~20% at IPO (Sep 2023) and ~25% in prior quarters |
| **CSS (Compute Subsystems)** | ~2x royalty rate vs. core-only licenses (stacks on top of Armv9 uplift) | 21 CSS licenses across 12 companies; 5 customers shipping CSS-based chips; top 4 Android OEMs all shipping CSS devices |
| **Data center core count increases** | More cores per chip = more royalty per chip | AWS Graviton5: 192 cores (2x Graviton4); NVIDIA Vera: 88 cores (up from 72 in Grace); Microsoft Cobalt 200: 132 cores |
| **Higher chip ASPs** | Arm captures value proportional to chip complexity | Premium smartphone SoCs, data center CPUs, automotive compute platforms |
| **Unit volume growth** | More chips shipped across all markets | Smartphone units flat to +2%, but data center, automotive, and IoT volumes growing |

**Smartphone royalties** surged ~30% YoY in Q4 FY2025 despite <2% unit growth, demonstrating the power of the price/mix shift from Armv9 and CSS.

**Data center royalties** grew triple digits YoY in Q3 FY2026, driven by hyperscaler deployment of Arm-based custom silicon and increasing core counts.

### 4b. Licensing Revenue -- Drivers & Model

License revenue is earned upfront or over time when customers sign agreements to access Arm's IP portfolio. Revenue is inherently lumpy quarter-to-quarter.

| Licensing Model | Description | FY2025 Status |
|-----------------|-------------|---------------|
| **Arm Total Access (ATA)** | Comprehensive access to Arm's full IP portfolio under a single agreement | 44 extant licenses (up from 31 in FY2024, +42% YoY) |
| **Arm Flexible Access (AFA)** | Modular access to select IP with low upfront commitment; royalties begin at production | 314 extant licenses (up from 222 in FY2024, +41% YoY) |
| **Custom / One-off licenses** | Bespoke agreements for specific IP or use cases | Includes sovereign AI deals (e.g., Malaysian Government multi-year agreement) |

**Key licensing revenue drivers:**
- Growing adoption of ATA and AFA models (combined license count grew ~40% YoY in FY2025)
- Sovereign AI compute frameworks (Malaysia proof-of-concept; France, Taiwan, India, Indonesia, Nigeria pursuing similar strategies)
- Custom silicon demand from hyperscalers (AWS, Google, Microsoft, Meta all designing Arm-based chips)
- First automotive CSS license signed in Q4 FY2025
- **Annualized Contract Value (ACV):** $1.37B in Q4 FY2025 (+15% YoY)
- **Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO):** $2.23B in Q4 FY2025 (-10% YoY, reflecting timing of large deals)

### 4c. Revenue Growth Forecast -- Arm's Own AGI CPU Initiative

At the March 2026 Arm Conference, the company announced its entry into the data center CPU market with its own **AGI CPU**:

| Metric | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Revenue start | Material revenue expected from FY2028 |
| Revenue target | ~$15 billion by FY2031 |
| First customer | Meta announced as anchor customer |
| Model shift | Represents a move from pure IP licensing to direct silicon sales |

This initiative, if successful, would fundamentally alter Arm's revenue composition from a pure royalty/licensing model toward a hybrid model with a direct silicon revenue stream.
