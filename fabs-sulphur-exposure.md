# TSMC & Memory Fabs: Exposure to Sulphur and Sulphuric Acid Amid the Iran Conflict

**Date:** March 19, 2026
**Sector:** Semiconductors / Specialty Chemicals
**Relevance:** TSMC (TSM), Samsung Electronics (005930.KS), SK Hynix (000660.KS), Micron (MU)

---

## Executive Summary

The US-Iran conflict that erupted on February 28, 2026 and the subsequent near-total closure of the Strait of Hormuz have exposed a critical and underappreciated vulnerability in the global semiconductor supply chain: dependence on sulphur and electronic-grade sulphuric acid (EGSA). While market attention has focused on oil prices and helium shortages, the disruption to sulphur flows -- a byproduct of Gulf petroleum refining that underpins wafer cleaning and etching processes at every leading-edge fab -- represents a slow-building but potentially severe threat to chip production.

The Gulf region accounts for ~44% of global sulphur exports, nearly twice its share of hydrocarbon output, because Gulf crude is particularly high in sulphur content. With tanker transits through the Strait collapsed by ~90% since March 2, the supply shock is already materialising: Chinese sulphur prices have surged 15% to a record CNY 4,650/t, and the global market faces a projected deficit of -5.13 million tonnes for 2026 -- the largest in over a decade. TSMC and Samsung collectively consume ~8,000 metric tonnes of ultra-high purity sulphuric acid annually, with demand growing ~15% year-on-year due to 3D stacking. Any sustained shortage threatens wafer cleaning yields and fab throughput at the world's most advanced nodes.

**Key conclusions:**

- **TSMC is the most exposed** among leading foundries due to Taiwan's dual vulnerability: reliance on LNG imports (only 8-11 days of reserves) for >50% of power generation, and dependence on Gulf-sourced sulphur for sulphuric acid feedstock.
- **Memory fabs face compounding risks:** SK Hynix and Samsung have lost >$200bn in combined market capitalisation since the war began, driven by helium supply disruption from Qatar (>33% of global supply offline) layered on top of sulphuric acid cost pressures.
- **The supply deficit was structural before the war.** Surging demand from Indonesian nickel processing (~10t sulphur per 1t nickel) and LFP battery production had already created a -5.13Mt gap. The Hormuz closure is an accelerant, not the root cause.
- **Mitigants exist but are slow-acting.** BASF's new Ludwigshafen EGSA plant won't start until 2027. Samsung has diversified via NES Materials (54,000 tpy facility). Alternative sulphur sources (copper smelter byproduct, pyrite ore) lack the purity for semiconductor applications without significant processing investment.

### 2.1 Production and Supply Chain

**Gulf Concentration Risk:** Saudi Arabia (7.2 Mt), UAE (6.3 Mt), and Qatar (3.1 Mt) alone account for ~16.6 Mt or ~20% of global production. However, their share of global *exports* is disproportionately higher at ~44%, because major producers like China and the US consume most output domestically.

### 2.2 Pre-War Structural Deficit

Even before the Hormuz closure, the global sulphur market faced a projected -5.13 Mt deficit for 2026 -- the largest in over a decade. Key demand drivers:

- **Indonesian nickel processing:** Requires ~10 tonnes of sulphur per 1 tonne of nickel produced. Indonesia's rapid nickel capacity expansion for EV batteries has absorbed enormous sulphur volumes, with ~75% of Indonesia's sulphur imported from the Middle East.
- **LFP battery production:** Lithium iron phosphate cathode manufacturing requires substantial sulphuric acid inputs. Global LFP capacity is scaling rapidly across China and emerging markets.
- **Semiconductor demand growth:** Advanced packaging (3D stacking, TSV structures) requires ~40% more sulphuric acid per wafer versus planar designs, driving ~15% annual demand growth for EGSA.

## 3. Sulphuric Acid in Semiconductor Manufacturing

### 3.1 Process Role: Piranha Solution (SPM Clean)

Sulphuric acid is indispensable in semiconductor fabrication, primarily through **piranha solution** (Sulphuric acid-Peroxide Mixture, or SPM): a 3:1 to 7:1 mixture of H2SO4 and H2O2. This is used at multiple stages:

- **Wafer cleaning:** Removes organic residues and contaminants before and after photolithography steps. Critical for maintaining yield at sub-7nm nodes.
- **Photoresist stripping:** Strips photoresist coatings after patterning, required at every lithography layer (advanced chips have 80+ layers).
- **Surface preparation:** Hydroxylates wafer surfaces for subsequent deposition steps.
- **Etching:** Selectively removes material in interconnect and via formation.

At advanced nodes (3nm, 2nm), a single wafer may undergo 20+ SPM cleaning steps. The acid must meet 5N purity (99.999%), with metallic impurities measured in parts per trillion (ppt). This ultra-high purity requirement means commodity-grade sulphuric acid is unsuitable -- only electronic-grade product from qualified suppliers can be used.

### 3.2 Consumption Volumes

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| TSMC + Samsung annual EGSA consumption | ~8,000 metric tonnes |
| YoY demand growth (3D stacking driven) | ~15% |
| Incremental acid per wafer for 3D vs planar | +40% |
| Global EGSA market value (2026) | ~$492 million |
| Semiconductor share of ultra-high purity H2SO4 | >60% of global consumption |

### 3.3 Supply Chain for Electronic-Grade Sulphuric Acid

EGSA production is concentrated among a small number of qualified suppliers:

| Supplier | Key Facilities | Capacity Notes |
|----------|---------------|----------------|
| **BASF** | Jiaxing, China (operational); Ludwigshafen, Germany (2027) | Leading global supplier; investing "high double-digit million euros" in new German plant |
| **KMG Chemicals** | US-based | Established semiconductor-grade supplier |
| **Avantor** | Global | Includes Puritan Products subsidiary |
| **NES Materials** | South Korea (54,000 tpy) | Samsung C&T holds 10% stake; began supplying Samsung in 2024 |
| **Namhae Chemical** | South Korea | Supplying prototype sulfuric acid to Samsung |

**Critical bottleneck:** While EGSA production can theoretically use any sulphur feedstock, qualification of new suppliers at leading-edge fabs takes 12-18 months due to stringent purity requirements and the need for extensive process validation. A sudden switch to new sulphur sources is not operationally feasible.

## 4. Company-Specific Exposure Analysis

### 4.1 TSMC (TSM) -- Highest Exposure

**Risk Level: HIGH**

TSMC faces the most acute exposure due to Taiwan's geographic and energy vulnerabilities:

- **Energy:** Taiwan sources 37% of electricity grid fuel from the Middle East and relies on LNG for >50% of power generation. Current LNG reserves cover only 8-11 days. A prolonged Hormuz closure threatens power stability for fabs running 24/7 at >90% utilisation.
- **Chemicals:** TSMC is the world's largest consumer of EGSA. Any shortage directly impacts SPM wafer cleaning, the most frequent wet process step. TSMC has not disclosed specific inventory levels or days-of-supply for key chemicals.
- **Concentration:** ~90% of the world's most advanced chips (<7nm) are manufactured in Taiwan, creating systemic risk from any disruption.
- **Stock impact:** TSM has declined ~7.7% over the past month, with a 5.5% single-day drop as Hormuz risks intensified.

**Mitigants:** TSMC's Arizona and Kumamoto (Japan) fabs provide some geographic diversification but account for minimal advanced-node capacity currently. TSMC has reportedly been building chemical inventories since Q4 2025 amid rising geopolitical awareness, though the adequacy of these stockpiles is unknown.

### 4.2 Samsung Electronics (005930.KS) -- High Exposure, Partial Mitigation

**Risk Level: HIGH (but with better mitigation than TSMC)**

- **Dual exposure:** Samsung operates both logic foundry and memory fabs, creating compound vulnerability to chemical shortages.
- **Sulphuric acid diversification:** Samsung has proactively diversified suppliers, with NES Materials (54,000 tpy facility, Samsung C&T holds 10% stake) and Namhae Chemical supplying domestically. This reduces but does not eliminate dependence on Gulf-sourced sulphur.
- **Helium:** South Korea is heavily exposed to Qatar helium supply disruption, affecting EUV lithography gas supply and fab cooling systems.
- **Market impact:** Samsung has lost significant market capitalisation since the war began, contributing to the >$200bn combined loss for Korean memory makers.

**Mitigants:** Domestic South Korean EGSA supply provides a buffer. Samsung's foundry business is smaller than TSMC's, reducing relative exposure per dollar of revenue. South Korea's strategic petroleum reserves and government coordination may extend LNG/energy runway beyond Taiwan's 8-11 day window.

### 4.3 SK Hynix (000660.KS) -- High Exposure

**Risk Level: HIGH**

- **Pure-play memory:** SK Hynix's entire revenue base is memory (DRAM/NAND), with no foundry diversification. Any chemical shortage directly impacts 100% of revenue.
- **HBM leadership:** As a leading supplier of HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) to Nvidia and AMD, SK Hynix operates at near-full utilisation for its most advanced products. HBM production uses advanced packaging with TSV technology, requiring ~40% more sulphuric acid per wafer.
- **Helium:** SK Hynix has stated it maintains "diverse supply chains and sufficient reserves" of helium, but has not quantified this.
- **Market impact:** Significant share price decline since the conflict began; exact figure combined with Samsung in >$200bn loss.

**Mitigants:** SK Hynix's Dalian (China) fab could potentially source EGSA from BASF's Jiaxing plant. The company's strong HBM position with Nvidia (sole supplier for HBM4) provides pricing power to pass through cost increases.

### 4.4 Micron Technology (MU) -- Moderate Exposure

**Risk Level: MODERATE**

- **Geographic advantage:** Micron's primary fabs are in the US (Boise, Virginia), Japan (Hiroshima), and Singapore -- all outside the Hormuz-dependent supply chain for energy.
- **Chemical sourcing:** US and Japanese EGSA supply from KMG, Avantor, and BASF (Jiaxing for Japan) provides alternative pathways not dependent on Gulf sulphur transit.
- **Indirect exposure:** Micron still faces rising input costs from global sulphur/sulphuric acid price increases and potential supply tightness.
- **Stock impact:** MU declined ~8.5% over 30 days, though strong fundamentals (137% revenue growth expected in Q2 2026; HBM supply sold out through 2027) provide a floor.

**Key risk:** Micron is excluded from Nvidia's HBM4 platform (Samsung and SK Hynix are sole suppliers), representing a structural loss of high-margin AI memory access starting late summer 2026. This is independent of the sulphur issue but compounds the risk profile.

## 5. Scenario Analysis

### Scenario 1: Strait Reopened Within 30 Days (Base Case -- 40% probability)

- US naval escorts and minesweeping operations restore partial shipping within 2-4 weeks.
- Sulphur flows resume at 50-70% of pre-war levels by mid-April.
- EGSA inventories at fabs draw down but do not deplete. Minor yield impacts, if any.
- **Impact:** Transitory cost increase of $50-100M across the industry. No production cuts. Stock recovery for TSM and memory names.

### Scenario 2: Prolonged Disruption, 60-90 Days (Bear Case -- 35% probability)

- Strait remains contested; intermittent convoy passages but no reliable commercial flow.
- Sulphur prices in China exceed CNY 6,000/t (+50% from current). EGSA spot prices double.
- Taiwan faces rolling power constraints as LNG reserves deplete. TSMC forced to prioritise most advanced nodes, reducing overall wafer starts by 10-15%.
- Memory fabs face helium rationing, reducing EUV lithography uptime by 15-20%.
- **Impact:** $5-15bn in lost semiconductor revenue industry-wide. TSMC could see 5-8% gross margin compression. Memory spot prices spike 30-50% on supply fears. TSM downside to $140-150 range; memory stocks face further 15-20% drawdown.

### Scenario 3: Escalation and Extended Closure, >90 Days (Tail Risk -- 25% probability)

- Conflict escalates with broader regional involvement. Strait effectively closed for 3+ months.
- Global sulphur deficit widens to >8 Mt. EGSA supply becomes critically constrained globally as non-Gulf sulphur is redirected to fertiliser and nickel (deemed more essential for food security and defense supply chains).
- Taiwan faces existential energy and chemical supply crisis. TSMC advanced-node production falls 30-50%.
- Global chip shortage comparable to or worse than 2020-2022 materialises within 60-90 days.
- **Impact:** Catastrophic for tech supply chains. $50-100bn+ in semiconductor industry revenue at risk. TSMC could face 15-20% revenue decline. Memory prices would spike 100%+, but production would also fall, creating a net revenue impact that depends on demand elasticity. Automotive, consumer electronics, and data centre buildouts would be severely impacted.

## 6. Alternative Supply Sources and Mitigants

### 6.1 Non-Gulf Sulphur Sources

| Source | Volume Available | Feasibility for EGSA |
|--------|-----------------|---------------------|
| US domestic (refineries) | 8.1 Mt production, but mostly consumed domestically | High purity achievable; limited export surplus |
| Canada (oil sands) | 5.0 Mt; significant stockpiles at Alberta oil sands sites | Potential emergency source; requires purification and logistics ramp |
| Russia | 7.5 Mt; subject to its own sanctions and geopolitical risks | Not viable for Western-aligned semiconductor supply chains |
| China domestic | 19 Mt; world's largest producer | Consumed internally; not a net exporter. Government may restrict exports |
| Kazakhstan | 4.8 Mt; Tengiz field expansion adding capacity | Landlocked; export logistics challenging |
| Copper smelter byproduct | Distributed globally; lower purity | Requires significant upgrading for semiconductor grade |

### 6.2 Timeline to Qualify New EGSA Supply

Qualification of a new EGSA supplier or feedstock source at a leading-edge fab requires:
- **6-12 months** for initial qualification testing
- **3-6 months** for process-of-record integration
- **Ongoing** statistical process control validation

This means that any emergency supply diversification initiated today would not yield qualified product until late 2026 at the earliest. The current crisis must be weathered with existing qualified supply chains and inventory.

### 6.3 Demand Reduction Levers

- **Recycling:** Some fabs are investing in on-site acid recycling/regeneration to extend supply. This can reduce virgin EGSA consumption by 20-30% but requires capital investment and process revalidation.
- **Process optimisation:** Reducing the number of SPM clean steps or extending bath life. This carries yield risk and is generally resisted by process engineers.
- **Alternative chemistries:** Ozone-based cleaning (DI water + O3) can substitute for some SPM steps but is less effective for heavy organic contamination and not universally qualified.

## 8. Appendix: Sulphuric Acid in Semiconductor Fabrication -- Technical Detail

### 8.1 Piranha Solution (SPM) Process

**Composition:** 3:1 to 7:1 ratio of H2SO4 to H2O2

**Reaction chemistry:**
- H2SO4 dehydrates H2O2, forming peroxysulfuric acid (Caro's acid, H2SO5)
- H2SO5 is a more powerful oxidant than H2O2 alone, generating atomic oxygen
- Organic contaminants are oxidised to CO2 and H2O
- Wafer surfaces are hydroxylated (--OH groups added), making them hydrophilic for subsequent processing

**Process parameters:**
- Temperature: Self-heating to >100C; controlled at 100-130C on hotplate for thorough cleaning
- Duration: 5-15 minutes per step
- Frequency: 20+ SPM steps per advanced-node wafer (each lithography layer requires pre- and post-clean)

### 8.2 Purity Requirements

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Overall purity | >99.999% (5N grade) |
| Metallic impurities (Fe, Cu, Na, K, etc.) | <10 parts per trillion (ppt) |
| Particle count (>0.2 micron) | <25 per mL |
| Non-volatile residue | <1 ppm |

These specifications are 100-1000x more stringent than industrial-grade sulphuric acid, which is why commodity sulphur/sulphuric acid cannot be directly substituted into semiconductor processes.
