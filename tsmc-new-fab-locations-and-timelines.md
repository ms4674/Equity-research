# TSMC New Fab Locations and Timelines

## Executive Summary

TSMC is executing the most aggressive global manufacturing expansion in semiconductor history, with capital expenditure rising from ~$41 billion in 2025 to a record $52-56 billion in 2026. The company is simultaneously building or ramping facilities across four countries (U.S., Japan, Germany, Taiwan) while scaling its advanced packaging footprint to meet surging AI demand. The centerpiece of the overseas strategy is Arizona, where TSMC plans up to 12 fabs, while domestically, four 2nm fabs are ramping with a target of 60,000+ wafers per month by end of 2026. A new 1.4nm mega-fab (Fab 25) broke ground in late 2025, targeting 2028 mass production.

## Japan — Kumamoto (JASM)

TSMC's Japan operations are run through **JASM** (Japan Advanced Semiconductor Manufacturing), a subsidiary. Combined investment across both fabs exceeds **$20 billion**, with significant Japanese government subsidies.

### Fab 1 — Operational

| Detail | Status |
|--------|--------|
| **Process** | 12/16nm and 22/28nm |
| **Status** | Mass production began late 2024 |
| **Capacity** | Target >100,000 wafers/month combined (Fab 1 + Fab 2) |

### Fab 2 — Groundbreaking Delayed, Major Upgrade Announced

| Detail | Status |
|--------|--------|
| **Original process** | 6nm/7nm |
| **Upgraded process** | 3nm — upgraded February 2026, marking the first time TSMC has exported cutting-edge mass production outside Taiwan |
| **Groundbreaking** | H2 2025 (postponed from Q1 2025) |
| **Equipment investment** | ~$17 billion |
| **Operations start** | End of 2027 |

The 3nm upgrade for Kumamoto Fab 2 is a strategically significant decision, driven by Japanese government industrial policy support and TSMC's need for geographic diversification of its most advanced nodes.

## Taiwan — Domestic Expansion

Taiwan remains the center of gravity for TSMC's most advanced manufacturing. Up to **ten fabs** are under construction or preparing operations across Taiwan's science parks in 2026.

### 2nm (N2) Expansion — Four Fabs Ramping

TSMC is bringing four 2nm fabrication lines online with a combined capacity target of **60,000+ wafers/month by end of 2026**, scaling toward **140,000 wafers/month by late 2026**.

#### Kaohsiung (Fab 22) — Nanzi Science Park

| Phase | Status | Capacity |
|-------|--------|----------|
| **P1** | Producing | ~10,000 wafers/month |
| **P2** | Equipment installed, trial wafers by end of 2025 | Combined P1+P2 target: >30,000 wpm |
| **P3** | Under construction | A16 (1.6nm) node |
| **P4-P5** | Preliminary / permit phase | Future expansion |

#### Hsinchu (Fab 20) — Baoshan District

| Phase | Status |
|-------|--------|
| **P1** | Completed risk-ramp testing; transitioning to full-scale production |
| **P2** | Fully equipped, awaiting qualification |
| **P3-P4** | Under construction for A14 (1.4nm) generation |

### 1.4nm (A14) Mega-Fab — Fab 25

| Detail | Status |
|--------|--------|
| **Location** | Central Taiwan Science Park (CTSP), near Taichung |
| **Investment** | NT$1.5 trillion (~$49 billion) |
| **Groundbreaking** | November 5, 2025 |
| **Scale** | Four separate processing plants |
| **EUV scanners** | 30+ during ramp-up |
| **Risk production** | End of 2027 |
| **Mass production** | H2 2028 |
| **Jobs created** | 8,000-10,000 |

A14 technology delivers 15% higher performance at the same power (or 30% lower power at the same performance) versus 2nm, with a 20% increase in logic density.

### Advanced Packaging Expansion

Advanced packaging is becoming a critical bottleneck and growth driver, particularly for AI accelerators. TSMC plans to **quadruple** CoWoS capacity.

| Metric | Late 2024 | End of 2026E |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| CoWoS wafers/month | ~35,000 | ~125,000-130,000 |

#### Key Packaging Facilities

| Facility | Location | Role |
|----------|----------|------|
| **AP3** | Longtan | Being upgraded with new tools |
| **AP6** | Zhunan | Fully operational since late 2024; initial capacity catalyst |
| **AP7** | Chiayi | World's largest advanced packaging hub; up to 8 production buildings; CoWoS-L and SoIC |
| **AP8** | Tainan | Repurposed from former display plant; heavy-duty production |
| **Arizona** | Phoenix, AZ | Two packaging plants planned alongside logic fabs |

TSMC is transitioning from CoWoS-S to **CoWoS-L**, which uses Local Silicon Interconnect bridges to stitch multiple chiplets together, enabling packages several times larger than prior generations.

Advanced packaging represented ~8% of TSMC revenue in 2025, projected to exceed 10% in 2026. Packaging capex is growing at a **24% CAGR** from 2025-2027.

## Investment Implications

1. **Capex intensity is structurally higher.** The jump from ~$30B (2024) to $52-56B (2026) reflects both leading-edge complexity and geographic diversification costs. Overseas fabs carry a 30-50% cost premium versus Taiwan.

2. **Arizona is the primary overseas bet.** With up to 12 fabs planned and $100B+ committed, Arizona is evolving from a political concession into a genuine strategic manufacturing hub. The acceleration of Fab 2 to 2027 signals real customer pull.

3. **Japan elevated to 3nm.** The Kumamoto Fab 2 upgrade to 3nm is a watershed moment — the first time TSMC has deployed cutting-edge mass production outside Taiwan. This de-risks concentration and deepens the Japan relationship.

4. **Europe stays mature-node.** ESMC Dresden is focused on automotive 28/22/16/12nm, reflecting the EU's supply-chain security priorities rather than leading-edge ambitions.

5. **Packaging is the next bottleneck.** Quadrupling CoWoS capacity by end of 2026 (35k to 130k wpm) is essential for AI accelerator supply. Nvidia reportedly controls most of 2026 CoWoS allocation.

6. **2nm ramp is unprecedented.** Four fabs simultaneously ramping 2nm in Taiwan, with 2nm revenue expected to surpass 3nm+5nm combined by Q3 2026, points to both customer demand strength and execution confidence.
