# CrowdStrike (CRWD) — Q4 FY2026 Earnings Note

**Report date:** March 3, 2026 (fiscal quarter ended January 31, 2026)

---

## Summary

CrowdStrike delivered a strong close to FY2026. Net new ARR of $331m in Q4 was a quarterly record, capping a full-year total that crossed $1b for the first time. Revenue growth re-accelerated to 23% y/y on a subscription base that is increasingly tilting toward adjacent modules — cloud, identity, and next-gen SIEM — which now collectively exceed an estimated $1.5b+ in ARR. Falcon Flex, the flexible consumption model, is proving to be a powerful upsell mechanism, with Flex-account ARR reaching $1.69b (+120% y/y). FY2027 guidance implies continued mid-20s ARR growth, with the emerging module mix likely to keep expanding as a share of total.

## Net New ARR

| Quarter | Net New ARR ($m) | Ending ARR ($b) | ARR y/y |
|---------|----------------:|----------------:|--------:|
| Q1 FY26 | 194 | 4.44 | ~22% |
| Q2 FY26 | 221 | 4.66 | 20% |
| Q3 FY26 | 265 | 4.92 | 23% |
| **Q4 FY26** | **331** | **5.25** | **24%** |
| **FY26 total** | **~1,010** | | |

Key observations:

- **Sequential acceleration throughout FY2026.** Net new ARR rose every quarter — $194m, $221m, $265m, $331m — a pattern the company has not produced since pre-COVID. Q4 net new ARR grew 47% y/y, comfortably above the "at least 50% H2 y/y growth" target management set at the Q3 call.
- **FY2026 was the first $1b+ net new ARR year.** For context, FY2025 delivered ~$810m in net new ARR. The ~25% y/y increase in annual net new ARR is notable given the law-of-large-numbers headwind at $4b+ starting ARR.
- **Falcon Flex is the primary mechanism.** Flex-account ending ARR hit $1.69b, up from ~$770m a year ago. Over 30% of total ARR now sits in Flex contracts, which bundle module access and create strong expansion dynamics. Management has cited Flex as the single largest contributor to net new ARR acceleration.
- **Gross retention held at ~97%.** The net new ARR gains are almost entirely expansion- and new-logo-driven, not retention improvement. This is the healthier composition.

### Net new ARR — what to watch in FY2027

FY2027 ARR guidance of $6.47–6.52b implies ~$1.22–1.27b in net new ARR, or ~20% growth on the FY2026 base. Management has historically guided conservatively on this metric. The key swing factor is whether Falcon Flex adoption continues at 100%+ growth or begins to moderate as the installed base converts.

## RPO (Remaining Performance Obligations)

CrowdStrike has historically disclosed RPO in its 10-K and 10-Q filings rather than in the earnings press release. The Q4 FY2026 10-K has not yet been filed as of this writing. Available context:

- **As of Q2 FY2025 (10-Q, July 2024),** total RPO was approximately $4.7b, with ~60% classified as current (to be recognized within 12 months). This implies average remaining contract duration of roughly 2.5–3 years.
- **Directionally, RPO should be growing faster than revenue.** The shift to Falcon Flex — which tends to carry longer, larger multi-module commitments — mechanically increases RPO. Flex total deal value exceeded $3.2b as of Q1 FY2026 and has been growing 5–6x y/y. Given that Flex deal value reflects the full contract commitment, RPO is likely tracking well above $6b by the end of FY2026.
- **Deferred revenue at end of FY2026 was $2.77b** (per balance sheet in the 8-K), up from $2.35b at end of FY2025, or +18% y/y — slightly below ARR growth, which can reflect timing of annual billing cycles.

### Why RPO matters here

The Falcon Flex model structurally lengthens contract commitments and front-loads the total deal value into RPO. This is a double-edged sword: it provides excellent forward visibility and reduces churn risk, but it also means that a slowdown in new Flex signings would show up in RPO deceleration before it appears in ARR. Investors should track the RPO-to-ARR ratio over time as a leading indicator of booking momentum.

## Core Endpoint Security vs. Adjacent Modules

This is the most important structural question for the CRWD thesis. The company is transitioning from a single-product endpoint leader to a multi-module platform, and the pace of that transition determines the long-term TAM and margin trajectory.

### Estimated ARR mix (as of end of FY2026)

| Category | Est. ARR ($b) | Est. % of total | Est. y/y growth |
|----------|-------------:|----------------:|----------------:|
| Core endpoint (EDR/EPP/XDR) | ~3.5–3.7 | ~67–70% | ~15–17% |
| Cloud security | ~0.7–0.8 | ~14–15% | ~35–40% |
| Identity protection | ~0.4–0.5 | ~8–9% | ~25–30% |
| Next-gen SIEM (LogScale) | ~0.5–0.6 | ~10–11% | ~80–100% |
| Other / Charlotte AI | <0.1 | <2% | n/m |

*Note: CrowdStrike does not officially break out ARR by module category. The above is estimated from disclosed data points, including management commentary that cloud + identity + SIEM exceeded $850m combined as of mid-FY2026 and $1.3b+ as of end of FY2025, combined with growth rates cited on earnings calls. Actual figures will vary.*

### Key takeaways on the mix shift

1. **Core endpoint is still growing double-digits, but it is no longer the primary growth engine.** At an estimated 15–17% y/y, the endpoint franchise is mature relative to the company average (24% total ARR growth). It is still gaining share — Gartner has CRWD as a clear leader in EPP — but the incremental TAM opportunity is smaller than in the adjacent categories.

2. **Adjacent modules are now contributing the majority of incremental net new ARR.** If core endpoint grew ~15% on a ~$3.2b FY2025 base, that implies ~$480–530m in endpoint net new ARR for FY2026. The remaining ~$480–530m of the $1.01b total came from cloud, identity, SIEM, and other modules — roughly a 50/50 split. This is a meaningful shift from FY2024, when endpoint was likely 60–65% of incremental ARR.

3. **Next-gen SIEM (LogScale) is the fastest-growing module.** Management has repeatedly highlighted SIEM as the biggest platform consolidation opportunity. LogScale competes directly with Splunk (now Cisco), and the value proposition — native integration with Falcon telemetry, lower cost, faster query performance — resonates strongly with security teams already running CrowdStrike on the endpoint. The ~$500m+ ARR run-rate (est.) makes this a meaningful contributor.

4. **Cloud security is the second pillar of the adjacent strategy.** Cloud workload protection, CSPM, and ASPM collectively approach ~$750m in ARR. Growth has been strong but is moderating from the 50%+ pace of FY2024–25. Competition from Wiz (acquired by Google) and Palo Alto's Prisma Cloud is real, but CrowdStrike's agent-based approach provides differentiated runtime protection.

5. **Identity is a steady grower, boosted by the FalconID launch.** The February 2026 launch of FalconID — extending Falcon into MFA and identity lifecycle management — opens a new sub-market. Combined with prior acquisitions (Preempt, Reposify), identity ARR is approaching ~$450m. The growth rate (~25–30%) is solid but not explosive; this is a market where incumbents (Okta, Microsoft Entra) are entrenched.

6. **Charlotte AI is nascent but strategically important.** CrowdStrike's AI-native SOC assistant is bundled in higher-tier SKUs and is not yet a material ARR contributor. The monetization path is through higher ASPs and faster time-to-value rather than standalone module pricing.

### Module adoption metrics

- **67% of $100k+ ARR customers use 5+ modules** (vs. ~62% a year ago)
- **48% use 6+ modules**
- **~60% of $100k+ customers on 8+ of the 28 available modules**
- Falcon Flex is accelerating multi-module adoption by removing per-module procurement friction

The deepening module adoption supports the thesis that CRWD's moat is shifting from "best EDR product" to "lowest-friction security platform." This makes the business stickier but also means future growth depends more on cross-sell execution than on winning new endpoint logos.

## Bottom Line

The Q4 print validates the platform thesis. Net new ARR acceleration throughout FY2026 was the strongest rebuttal to post-July-2024-outage demand concerns. The mix shift toward adjacent modules — now roughly half of incremental net new ARR — extends the growth runway beyond endpoint market saturation. RPO trends (once disclosed in the 10-K) will be an important confirmation; Falcon Flex's long-duration contracts should push RPO growth above ARR growth. The key risk is execution in cloud and SIEM, where well-capitalized competitors are investing aggressively. At current levels, the stock prices in sustained 20%+ growth — which the data supports, but which requires continued cross-sell discipline.
