# CrowdStrike (CRWD) — Q4 FY2026 Earnings Note

**Quarter ended:** January 31, 2026
**Report date:** March 3, 2026

---

## Summary

CrowdStrike delivered a strong finish to FY2026, beating consensus on both revenue and EPS. The headline story is the continued acceleration in net new ARR, which reached $330.7M in Q4 (+47% YoY), bringing full-year net new ARR to a record $1.01B. Revenue of $1.31B grew 23% YoY. The quarter validates that the July 2024 outage is firmly in the rearview and that the Falcon Flex licensing model is driving meaningful platform consolidation. FY2027 guidance was broadly in line with expectations, suggesting management is setting up for a beat-and-raise cadence rather than pulling forward upside.

---

## Net New ARR: Accelerating Through the Year

| Period | Net New ARR ($M) | YoY Growth |
|--------|----------------:|------------|
| Q1 FY2026 | 193.8 | ~16% |
| Q2 FY2026 | 221.0 | ~32% |
| Q3 FY2026 | 265.3 | 73% |
| Q4 FY2026 | 330.7 | 47% |
| **Full Year FY2026** | **~1,011** | **Record** |

Net new ARR accelerated through the first three quarters before moderating slightly in Q4, though the Q4 figure is the largest absolute dollar add in company history. The sequential step-up from Q3 to Q4 of ~$65M suggests sustained deal momentum, likely aided by fiscal year-end budget flush dynamics and the growing pull of Falcon Flex.

The Q4 YoY comp (47% vs Q3's 73%) reflects a tougher prior-year base rather than any fundamental deceleration. On a two-year stacked basis, net new ARR is running well above historical trend.

Ending ARR reached $5.25B, up 24% YoY, re-accelerating from 22-23% growth earlier in FY2026.

**Key question for FY2027:** Management's ARR guidance of $6.47-6.52B implies ~$1.22-1.27B in net new ARR, or roughly 20-25% growth off the $1.01B base. This looks achievable given current momentum, but continuation of sequential acceleration from here becomes harder on sheer scale.

## Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO)

CrowdStrike has historically disclosed RPO in its 10-Q/10-K filings rather than in the earnings press release. At the time of this note, the Q4 FY2026 10-K has not yet been filed, so exact RPO figures are pending.

However, several proxies point to robust backlog growth:

- **Falcon Flex deal value:** Flex accounts exceeded $3.2B in total deal value as of mid-FY2026, growing >6x YoY. By Q4, Flex ending ARR reached $1.69B, up >120% YoY. Flex contracts tend to be multi-year with significant committed spend, which directly feeds into RPO.
- **Deferred revenue + backlog dynamics:** Subscription revenue of $1.24B in Q4 grew 23% YoY, roughly in line with ARR growth, suggesting healthy conversion of backlog to recognized revenue.
- **Cash flow as a leading indicator:** Q4 operating cash flow of $497.9M and FY2026 OCF of $1.61B (33% of revenue) reflect strong upfront billing on multi-year contracts. Free cash flow of $1.24B for the year implies billings are running well ahead of revenue recognition.

**Implications:** When Q4 RPO figures are disclosed (likely in the 10-K), we expect them to show meaningful sequential and year-over-year growth, driven by Falcon Flex's multi-year, high-value contract structure. RPO growth outpacing ARR growth would confirm lengthening contract durations and improving revenue visibility.

## Core Endpoint Security vs. Adjacent Modules

This is the most important structural story within the quarter. CrowdStrike is evolving from an endpoint security vendor into a broad cybersecurity platform, and the economics of each segment differ materially.

### Core Endpoint Protection

- CrowdStrike remains the market leader in endpoint protection, named a Leader in the Gartner Magic Quadrant for Endpoint Protection Platforms for the sixth consecutive year (placed highest for Ability to Execute and furthest right for Completeness of Vision).
- Management reported **acceleration** in endpoint ARR growth in Q3 FY2026, suggesting the core business is not ceding share to adjacent expansion. This is notable — in many platform stories, the core decelerates as new modules drive the incremental growth.
- Endpoint remains the wedge product and primary land motion. New logo acquisition continues to be driven largely by endpoint, after which customers expand into adjacent modules via Falcon Flex.
- Estimated endpoint ARR: While CrowdStrike does not break out endpoint-specific ARR, by backing out the combined adjacent module disclosure (~$1.56B as of Q2 for Cloud + Identity + SIEM, likely approaching ~$1.8-2.0B by Q4), the implied endpoint/core ARR is roughly $3.3-3.5B, or ~63-66% of total ending ARR. This share has been declining as adjacent modules scale faster, but the absolute dollar base continues to grow.

### Adjacent Modules (Cloud Security, Identity, Next-Gen SIEM)

| Module | ARR (as of Q2 FY2026) | YoY Growth |
|--------|----------------------:|------------|
| Cloud Security | >$700M | ~35% |
| Next-Gen Identity | $435M | ~21% |
| Combined (Cloud + Identity + SIEM) | $1.56B | ~40% |

- **Cloud Security** is the fastest-growing adjacent module, bolstered by the Onum acquisition and rising demand to secure cloud-native and AI workloads. At 35% growth, it is outpacing total company ARR growth by ~12 points.
- **Next-Gen Identity** (Falcon Identity Protection) has been a consistent grower but at a more moderate pace (~21% YoY). Identity is a strategic pillar given the convergence of identity and endpoint in zero-trust architectures, but faces competition from pure-play identity vendors.
- **Next-Gen SIEM** (LogScale/Falcon SIEM) is the newest major module and potentially the largest long-term TAM expansion. CrowdStrike's approach — leveraging the data already flowing through Falcon — gives it a structural cost and performance advantage over legacy SIEM solutions. Exact standalone ARR is not disclosed, but the combined trio's 40% growth rate implies SIEM is contributing meaningfully.
- **Exposure Management** and **Charlotte AI** are emerging product areas. Charlotte AI adoption is being bundled into Falcon Flex deals and is not yet separately quantifiable.

### Module Adoption Depth

| Metric | Percentage of Customers |
|--------|------------------------:|
| 5+ modules | ~65% (est.) |
| 6+ modules | 48% |
| 7+ modules | 33% |
| 8+ modules | 23% |

Multi-module adoption continues to deepen. The 8+ module cohort growing to 23% is significant — these customers represent the highest-value, lowest-churn accounts and are the clearest evidence that the platform consolidation strategy is working.

### Growth Mix Implications

The adjacent modules are likely contributing the majority of net new ARR growth in dollar terms. With the combined Cloud/Identity/SIEM bucket growing at ~40% off a ~$1.56B base (as of Q2), these modules are generating roughly $450-500M in net new ARR annually, or close to half of the $1.01B full-year total. The remainder (~$500-550M) is attributable to endpoint and other core modules.

This mix shift has positive implications for the medium-term growth trajectory: as adjacent modules scale toward $3B+ in combined ARR over the next two to three years, they provide a durable growth engine even if endpoint growth moderates toward the mid-teens.

## Falcon Flex: The Mechanism Behind Consolidation

Falcon Flex is the single most important go-to-market innovation driving both net new ARR and module adoption:

- **$1.69B in ending ARR** from Flex accounts in Q4, growing >120% YoY
- Over 1,000 Flex customers with average ending ARR exceeding $1M per customer
- Flex deal value exceeded $3.2B by mid-FY2026, growing >6x YoY
- Flex contracts allow customers to deploy any combination of Falcon modules under a unified subscription, reducing procurement friction and accelerating time-to-value

Flex is a critical mechanism because it transforms the expansion motion: instead of selling individual modules in separate procurement cycles, customers commit to a platform spend level and then activate modules as needed. This front-loads contract value (boosting RPO and cash flow) while enabling faster module adoption (boosting multi-module attach rates).

## GAAP Profitability: Inflection Quarter

Q4 FY2026 marked a milestone: CrowdStrike posted its first quarter of GAAP net income at $38.7M, swinging from a GAAP net loss of $92.3M in Q4 FY2025 — a $131M improvement year-over-year. This is a meaningful signal for a company that has historically operated at a GAAP loss despite strong non-GAAP profitability.

### Quarterly GAAP vs. Non-GAAP Trend

| Period | GAAP Net Income (Loss) | Non-GAAP Net Income | Gap (SBC + Other) |
|--------|----------------------:|--------------------|-------------------:|
| Q3 FY2025 | ($16.8M) | — | — |
| Q4 FY2025 | ($92.3M) | $260.9M | ~$353M |
| Q3 FY2026 | ($34.0M) | $245.4M | ~$279M |
| Q4 FY2026 | **$38.7M** | $289.1M | ~$250M |

| Period | GAAP Operating Income (Loss) | Non-GAAP Operating Income |
|--------|----------------------------:|-------------------------:|
| Q3 FY2025 | ($55.7M) | $200.7M |
| Q3 FY2026 | ($69.4M) | $264.6M |
| Q4 FY2025 | ($85.3M) | — |
| Q4 FY2026 | Positive (implied by NI) | — |

For the full year, non-GAAP net income reached $956.6M ($3.73 per diluted share), up significantly from FY2025. The full-year GAAP figures will be disclosed in the 10-K, but given Q4's positive print, FY2026 GAAP net loss should be materially narrower than FY2025.

### The SBC Question

The dominant driver of the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap remains stock-based compensation. In Q4, the gap of ~$250M implies an annualized SBC run-rate of roughly $1B, or approximately 21% of revenue. This is elevated relative to mature software companies but has been compressing as revenue scales:

- SBC intensity has been declining as a percentage of revenue as the top line grows 22-23% while SBC grows more slowly. This operating leverage is what enabled the Q4 GAAP crossover.
- The Q4 FY2025 gap was ~$353M, narrowing to ~$250M in Q4 FY2026 — a ~$100M compression in four quarters. If this pace continues, GAAP profitability should become the norm rather than the exception by FY2027.

Beyond SBC, the non-GAAP reconciliation also excludes amortization of acquired intangibles, acquisition-related expenses, and residual July 2024 Incident costs. CrowdStrike adopted a 21.0% long-term non-GAAP tax rate in FY2026 (down from 22.5%), and beginning in FY2026, employer payroll taxes on stock-based awards are also excluded from non-GAAP figures.

### Cash Flow vs. GAAP Earnings

The real profitability story may be better told through cash flow. FY2026 operating cash flow of $1.61B and free cash flow of $1.24B represent OCF and FCF margins of 33% and 26%, respectively. These are among the best in enterprise software and demonstrate that the underlying economic model is highly profitable regardless of GAAP accounting treatment of SBC.

| Metric | Q4 FY2026 | FY2026 |
|--------|----------:|-------:|
| Operating Cash Flow | $497.9M | $1.61B |
| Free Cash Flow | $376.4M | $1.24B |
| OCF Margin | ~38% | ~33% |
| FCF Margin | ~29% | ~26% |

The gap between FCF and GAAP net income is expected to narrow as SBC intensity continues to decline, which should make CrowdStrike increasingly attractive to investors who screen on GAAP profitability or who weight SBC as a real economic cost.

### Implications

1. **Index/benchmark eligibility:** Sustained GAAP profitability could improve CrowdStrike's eligibility or weighting in certain indices that screen on GAAP earnings, potentially creating a passive flow tailwind.
2. **Investor base broadening:** The GAAP crossover should attract traditional value-oriented and GARP investors who have historically avoided GAAP-unprofitable software names.
3. **SBC trajectory is the key variable:** If SBC as a percentage of revenue continues to compress by 2-3 points annually, CrowdStrike could sustain consistent GAAP profitability by FY2027-2028 even without any acceleration in top-line growth.
4. **Cash flow remains king:** With FCF margins already at ~26%, the business generates substantial real cash regardless of GAAP optics. The GAAP crossover is important for narrative and eligibility purposes but does not change the fundamental cash economics.

## Key Debates and Risks

1. **Endpoint maturity vs. platform TAM expansion:** Bulls see the adjacent modules as extending CrowdStrike's growth runway; bears worry that endpoint (the core cash cow) could slow as the market matures and competitors (Microsoft, SentinelOne) increase pressure.

2. **Falcon Flex pull-forward risk:** Flex's multi-year structure front-loads ARR and cash flow, but creates a question about whether current net new ARR includes demand that would have materialized in later periods. A slowdown in new Flex deal signings would pressure net new ARR growth.

3. **RPO composition and duration:** When Q4 RPO is disclosed, watch for (a) total RPO growth vs. ARR growth, and (b) the current vs. non-current mix. A shift toward longer-duration RPO would be bullish for revenue visibility but could also signal customer lock-in concerns.

4. **SIEM competitive dynamics:** The next-gen SIEM market is intensely competitive (Splunk/Cisco, Elastic, Palo Alto XSIAM). CrowdStrike's data advantage is real, but scaling SIEM requires displacing entrenched incumbents.

5. **Valuation:** At ~15-16x forward revenue, CrowdStrike trades at a premium to most security peers. Sustained 20%+ growth and expanding margins justify the multiple, but any net new ARR deceleration would pressure the stock.

## Bottom Line

Q4 FY2026 confirms CrowdStrike's position as the best-in-class cybersecurity platform compounder. Net new ARR of $330.7M (+47% YoY) and full-year net new ARR surpassing $1B are clear evidence that platform consolidation — driven by Falcon Flex — is the dominant force in enterprise security spending. The growth mix is shifting toward adjacent modules (Cloud, Identity, SIEM), which are collectively growing at ~40% and now represent an estimated one-third of total ARR, providing a structural growth tailwind. RPO details from the forthcoming 10-K filing will be important to confirm the durability of the backlog build, but strong cash flow dynamics already suggest favorable trends. FY2027 guidance looks conservative and sets up for multiple raises through the year.
