# Social Media Regulation for Under-16s in Europe

## Overview

As of March 2026, a wave of social media restrictions targeting children under 16 is sweeping across Europe. Australia's December 2025 ban on social media for under-16s catalyzed European action, with multiple EU and non-EU countries now enacting or proposing similar legislation. The European Parliament has called for a harmonized EU-wide minimum age of 16 for social media, with parental consent allowed for ages 13-16.

---

## EU-Level Developments

| Date | Development |
|------|------------|
| Nov 2025 | European Parliament passed a **non-binding resolution** calling for a minimum age of **16** for social media and video-sharing platforms, with access for ages 13-16 only with parental consent |
| Feb 2026 | European Commission published an action plan against cyberbullying and convened an **expert panel** to advise on social media age restrictions |
| Mar 2026 | EU leaders at the European Council **urged** the introduction of a minimum age for social media platforms, calling on the Commission to implement the Digital Services Act (DSA) guidelines on protecting minors |
| Summer 2026 (expected) | Expert panel to deliver recommendations on whether to implement EU-wide age restrictions |

The EU is also developing a **privacy-preserving age-verification tool** aligned with the European Digital Identity Wallet (eIDAS) framework.

## GDPR Digital Age of Consent Map (Baseline)

The GDPR (Article 8) sets a default digital age of consent at 16, but allows Member States to lower it to no less than 13. Current settings:

| Age | Countries |
|-----|-----------|
| **13** | Belgium, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Latvia, Malta, Portugal, Sweden, UK |
| **14** | Austria, Bulgaria, Cyprus, Italy, Lithuania, Spain |
| **15** | Czech Republic, France, Greece |
| **16** | Croatia, Germany, Hungary, Ireland, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Poland, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia |

> **Note:** Many countries with a GDPR age of 13 are now proposing much higher thresholds (15-16) through new dedicated social media legislation, superseding the baseline GDPR consent framework.

## The "Coalition of the Digitally Willing"

Spain initiated a **"Coalition of the Digitally Willing"** — a group of European countries coordinating cross-border regulation of social media for children.

**Founding members (6):** France, Denmark, Greece, Austria, Portugal, Spain

**Additional supporters (9):** Cyprus, Slovenia, Italy, Germany, Netherlands, Belgium, Slovakia, Croatia (joined by June 2025)

### Coalition's Key Proposals
- Ban or restrict under-16 access with effective age verification
- Make platform executives legally liable for failing to remove illegal content
- Criminalize algorithmic manipulation and amplification of illegal content
- Mandate age verification and parental controls on all internet-connected devices sold in Europe
- Establish a European "digital age of majority"
- Use the EU Digital Identity Wallet for age verification

## Key Platforms Affected

The bans and proposed legislation generally target platforms where users can create public profiles, interact with others, and share content without editorial control. This includes:

- **TikTok** — Cited as a primary concern due to addictive recommendation algorithms and autoplay
- **Instagram** / **Facebook** (Meta)
- **Snapchat**
- **YouTube** (in some proposals)
- **X** (formerly Twitter)

### Common Exemptions
- Messaging apps (WhatsApp, Signal, etc.)
- Email services
- School and educational platforms
- E-commerce services
- Video games (in some proposals)
- Job/housing listing platforms

## Enforcement Approaches

| Approach | Countries |
|----------|-----------|
| **Platform-side age verification** (platforms bear responsibility) | Spain, Portugal, Norway, Poland, Australia model |
| **Digital Identity Wallet / eIDAS** | Portugal, EU-level proposal |
| **Parental consent verification** | France, Denmark, Germany, Portugal (13-16), Montenegro |
| **Fines for non-compliance** | Poland, Montenegro (up to 10% global revenue), EU-level DSA |
| **Executive liability** | Spain (proposed) |
