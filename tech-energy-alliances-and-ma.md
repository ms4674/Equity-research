# Tech Giants Energy Alliances & M&A: Microsoft, Meta, Google, and OpenAI

## Executive Summary

The explosive growth of AI workloads has triggered an unprecedented wave of energy deals by the largest technology companies. Microsoft, Meta, Google/Alphabet, and OpenAI have collectively committed to tens of gigawatts of new power capacity through a diverse mix of nuclear restarts, advanced reactor development, renewable PPAs, geothermal/fusion bets, and outright acquisitions. U.S. data center power demand is projected to surge from ~62 GW in 2025 to ~134 GW by 2030, and global data center electricity consumption is forecast to rise from 460 TWh (2024) to 1,300 TWh (2035). This document catalogs every major energy alliance, power purchase agreement, and M&A transaction announced by these four companies from 2024 through early 2026.

## 4. OpenAI

### 4.1 Stargate Initiative

The Stargate project is a $500 billion multi-year initiative announced in January 2025 to build 10 GW of U.S. AI infrastructure by 2029, backed by OpenAI, SoftBank, and Oracle.

| Deal | Partner(s) | Capacity | Investment | Status |
|------|-----------|----------|------------|--------|
| SB Energy data center (Milam County, TX) | SoftBank / SB Energy | 1.2 GW | $500M each from OpenAI and SoftBank + $800M from Ares | Announced Jan 2026; facilities expected to enter service 2026 |
| Stargate Wisconsin campus | Oracle, Vantage Data Centers | ~1 GW (4 data centers) | $15B+ | Port Washington, WI; completion target 2028 |
| Stargate Michigan campus | Oracle, Related Digital | >1 GW | Multi-billion | Saline Township, MI; construction began early 2026 |
| NVIDIA systems deployment | NVIDIA | 10 GW of NVIDIA systems | NVIDIA to invest up to $100B as systems deploy | Announced Sep 2025; first GW targeted H2 2026 |

**Total planned Stargate capacity: >8 GW across all U.S. sites**

### 4.2 Energy Community Commitments

OpenAI unveiled its "Stargate Community Plan" in January 2026, committing to:
- Fund new power generation and transmission resources at each site
- Provide dedicated power supplies and battery storage
- Ensure local electricity costs do not rise for existing ratepayers
- Key utility partners: DTE Energy (Michigan), local utilities (Wisconsin)

### 4.3 Summary Metrics

- **Total infrastructure investment**: $500B initiative (with SoftBank and Oracle)
- **Total planned capacity**: >8 GW across Texas, Wisconsin, Michigan, and additional sites
- **Direct energy investment**: $1B into SB Energy
- **Strategy**: Build-to-suit data centers with co-located or dedicated power; utility partnerships to avoid rate impact

## 6. Key Themes

### 6.1 From PPAs to Ownership
Google's $4.75B acquisition of Intersect Power signals a shift from the traditional power purchase agreement model toward vertical integration. Grid interconnection queues (2,300 GW backlog, ~20% completion rate) are driving hyperscalers to own generation assets directly.

### 6.2 Nuclear Renaissance
All four companies (directly or indirectly) are betting on nuclear power. Microsoft restarted Three Mile Island, Meta committed to 6.6 GW of nuclear, Google invested in SMRs and a plant restart, and OpenAI's Stargate partners are pursuing nuclear options. Big tech has contracted for over 10 GW of new U.S. nuclear capacity in the past two years.

### 6.3 Frontier Energy Bets
Microsoft is uniquely positioned with a fusion energy PPA (Helion), while Meta and Google are both investing in next-generation geothermal (Sage and Fervo, respectively). These frontier bets hedge against the multi-year timelines of nuclear deployment.

### 6.4 Scale of Capital Deployment
The combined capital being deployed is staggering: Microsoft's $10B+ Brookfield deal, Google's $4.75B Intersect acquisition, OpenAI's $500B Stargate initiative, and Meta's multi-GW nuclear portfolio represent the largest corporate energy procurement wave in history.

### 6.5 Community and Regulatory Engagement
Both Microsoft and OpenAI have made explicit commitments that data center energy procurement will not raise local electricity rates. This reflects growing regulatory and community scrutiny of the grid impact of AI data centers.
