# Made in China 2030: The AI Pivot

**Equity Research — Thematic Overview**
**May 2026**

---

## Executive Summary

China's industrial strategy has evolved decisively from the original "Made in China 2025" manufacturing upgrade plan into a broader, AI-centric national program targeting 2030. The 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030) positions artificial intelligence as the single most important technology vector across the economy — from semiconductors and robotics to autonomous vehicles and public governance. Backed by trillions of yuan in fiscal commitments, an increasingly capable domestic chip ecosystem, and breakthroughs like DeepSeek, China is building a parallel AI stack designed to function independently of Western technology. This writeup maps the policy architecture, identifies the key investable verticals, profiles the leading companies, and assesses the risks.

---

## 1. Policy Architecture: From MiC 2025 to AI+ 2030

### 1.1 Evolution of the Strategy

The original Made in China 2025 plan, launched in 2015, focused on moving Chinese manufacturing up the value chain in ten priority sectors. That plan's targets — 70% domestic content in core components by 2025 — were partially met in some areas (solar, batteries, EVs) and fell short in others (semiconductors, advanced machine tools).

The successor framework is not branded as "Made in China 2030" officially, but the strategic substance is clear across three interlocking policy documents:

| Document | Released | Core Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Next Generation AI Development Plan | 2017 (updated 2024) | World-leading AI status by 2030 |
| "AI+" Action Plan | 2025 | AI integration across six priority domains |
| 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030) | March 2026 | AI + semiconductors at the center of tech self-reliance |

### 1.2 The "AI+" Framework

The State Council's "AI+" action plan sets explicit adoption milestones:

- **By 2027:** AI integration across six major sectors; intelligent terminals and smart agents at >70% adoption; 1,000 industrial intelligent agents deployed; 500 application scenarios demonstrated.
- **By 2030:** AI becomes a primary growth engine; intelligent terminals and agents at >90% adoption; full integration into high-quality economic development.
- **By 2035:** Transition to an "intelligent economy and smart society" stage.

The six priority domains are: (1) Science & technology, (2) Industrial development, (3) Consumer services, (4) Public services, (5) Governance, and (6) Global cooperation.

### 1.3 Fiscal Commitment

The 2026 budget architecture signals the scale of the state's ambition:

- **1.3 trillion yuan** in ultra-long special bonds earmarked for strategic technology
- **7 trillion yuan** directed toward power grids and AI computing infrastructure
- **800 billion yuan** allocated directly to major national strategic projects
- **R&D spending** targeted to increase >7% annually through 2030

The government aims to grow semiconductors and intelligent robots into a combined "10 trillion yuan" strategic industry by 2030.

## 2. The Domestic AI Stack

### 2.1 Foundation Models: DeepSeek and the Open-Source Wave

DeepSeek's R1 model (January 2025) was a watershed moment. By achieving near-frontier reasoning performance at a reported development cost of ~$6 million — a fraction of comparable U.S. efforts — it demonstrated that algorithmic efficiency could partially compensate for hardware constraints. The market impact was immediate: Nvidia lost $593 billion in market value in a single session.

Since then, the competitive dynamics have accelerated:

- **DeepSeek** commands ~89% market share among Chinese AI users. V4 (April 2026) introduced a one-million-token context window at lower inference costs.
- **Alibaba's Qwen** family has been integrated across Alibaba's commerce, cloud, and consumer ecosystem. Qwen models lead in cumulative open-source downloads.
- **ByteDance's Doubao** reached 83 million MAU, far exceeding Baidu's ERNIE Bot at 23 million.
- **Baidu** released ERNIE 5.0 (November 2025) and continues to build on its cloud AI platform (30 billion yuan cloud AI revenue in 2025, subscription revenue up 143% YoY).

Chinese models collectively surpassed Western counterparts in cumulative open-source downloads as of August 2025, with strong adoption across the Global South, Southeast Asia, and Africa.

**Investment implication:** The foundation model layer in China is rapidly commoditizing. Value creation will accrue to companies that best integrate models into applications and services rather than to model developers per se. Alibaba and ByteDance are better positioned than pure-play model labs.

### 2.2 Semiconductors: The Bottleneck That Defines Everything

China's AI ambitions ultimately rest on its ability to manufacture advanced chips domestically. The 2030 semiconductor self-reliance strategy is the most capital-intensive component of the entire program.

**Current state of domestic production:**

| Metric | Current | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Advanced wafer output | ~20,000 wafers/month | 100,000 (near-term); 500,000 (by 2030) |
| Cambricon accelerator output | ~150,000 units (2025) | 500,000 units (2026) |
| Huawei Ascend output | Doubling planned | Undisclosed |
| SMIC advanced node yield | ~20% | Needs significant improvement |
| Combined capex (2025) | ~$98 billion | Escalating |

**Key players in the domestic chip stack:**

- **SMIC** (0981.HK): China's most advanced foundry, operating N+2 (7nm-class) process. Severe yield challenges (~20% vs. TSMC's 60%+) and equipment restrictions limit throughput. Multiple Chinese AI chipmakers — Cambricon, Biren, Moore Threads, Alibaba, Baidu, Huawei — compete for the same constrained capacity.
- **Cambricon** (688256.SS): China's leading AI chip pure-play. Plans to triple output to 500,000 accelerators in 2026 (Siyuan 590/690). Revenue is scaling rapidly, though profitability remains distant given yield economics.
- **Huawei (HiSilicon/Ascend)**: Developing a 5nm process pathway using DUV lithography as a sanctions-proof production route. The Ascend 910B/C series is the de facto domestic alternative to Nvidia A100/H100 for training workloads.
- **CXMT / YMTC**: Memory manufacturers working to close the gap in DRAM and NAND, though HBM (high-bandwidth memory) supply remains a critical bottleneck — Chinese firms still depend on SK Hynix and Samsung.

**Investment implication:** The semiconductor supply chain is the highest-conviction, longest-duration theme. Equipment makers (domestic lithography, etching, deposition), foundry capacity expansions, and advanced packaging are the structural beneficiaries. However, yield economics mean that China's chips will remain more expensive per unit of compute than TSMC-fabricated alternatives for the foreseeable future — a tax on every downstream AI application.

### 2.3 Embodied AI and Humanoid Robotics

The 15th Five-Year Plan explicitly calls out "embodied intelligence" and "swarm intelligence" as priority development areas. China's humanoid robotics sector has moved from R&D curiosity to early commercial deployment in under two years.

**UBTECH Robotics** (9880.HK):
- 2025 revenue: 2.0 billion yuan (+53% YoY)
- Industrial humanoid revenue: 820 million yuan (20x increase)
- Delivered >1,000 full-size industrial humanoids — first company globally to cross this threshold
- Deployments: automotive, logistics, electronics, semiconductors, aerospace
- Signed Airbus service agreement (early 2026) for aviation manufacturing
- Gross margin: 54.6% on humanoid segment
- R&D: 507 million yuan (25.4% of revenue), 942 engineers
- Proprietary stack: Thinker VLA model, Group Brain Network 2.0, multi-robot coordination

**Unitree Robotics** (pre-IPO):
- Shipped 5,500+ non-full-size humanoids in 2025 (~70% exported to universities/research)
- Production cost: 60,000–70,000 yuan per unit; gross margin: 62.9%
- Plans to allocate 2 billion yuan from IPO proceeds toward robot "brain" development
- R&D: 90–120 million yuan annually, ~175 engineers

Both companies are transitioning from motion-control hardware to full-stack autonomy — perception, cognition, and decision-making. The strategic direction mirrors the broader "AI+" thesis: hardware is the entry point, but software intelligence is the value driver.

**Other notable players:** Galbot, AgiBot (Zhiyuan Robotics), Fourier Intelligence, Kepler Robotics.

**Investment implication:** Humanoid robotics is the earliest-stage vertical in this report but has the most asymmetric upside if deployment scales. UBTECH's industrial traction is real but the market is pricing in aggressive forward growth. Unitree's IPO will be a key event. Watch for government procurement mandates as a demand catalyst.

### 2.4 Autonomous Driving and Intelligent Vehicles

China's NEV (New Energy Vehicle) sector — already the world's largest — is becoming the primary deployment platform for edge AI.

**BYD** (1211.HK / 002594.SZ):
- God's Eye intelligent driving system launched February 2025; updated to God's Eye 5.0 (January 2026)
- 2.3 million vehicles equipped by end-2025, generating 160 million km of driving data daily
- Three-tier hardware stack (DiPilot 600/300/100) spanning luxury to mainstream price points
- Democratized intelligent driving: 21 models priced 70,000–200,000 yuan ($9,650–$27,500) with full ADAS
- NVIDIA DRIVE partnership for in-vehicle compute and cloud training

**Baidu Apollo:**
- 20 million cumulative robotaxi rides; 300 million km of autonomous operation (as of February 2026)
- 3.4 million trips in Q4 2025 alone (+200% YoY)
- L4 autonomous driving technology deployed in Wuhan, Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen, and Chongqing

**Other key players:** Xpeng (targeting L3 by late 2025), Li Auto (L4 vehicle within three years), Huawei (HiPhi/Arcfox intelligent driving solutions), Pony.ai (IPO completed 2024).

BYD's approach is particularly significant because it collapses the cost barrier. By integrating intelligent driving into vehicles priced under $15,000, BYD is generating training data at a scale that no Western automaker can match — a classic data flywheel.

**Investment implication:** BYD's scale advantage in data generation, vertical integration (including its own semiconductor subsidiary), and cost leadership makes it the most defensible AI play within the auto sector. Baidu Apollo is the pure-play autonomous driving bet but lacks a clear path to profitability.

## 3. The Export Control Overhang

U.S. semiconductor export controls remain the single largest exogenous risk to China's AI timeline. The policy landscape as of mid-2026:

- **Case-by-case licensing** (since January 2026) replaced the blanket presumption of denial for certain chips below defined performance thresholds (TPP <21,000; DRAM bandwidth <6,500 GB/s). NVIDIA H200 and AMD MI325X are eligible for individual review.
- **25% tariff** on H200 and MI325X chips imposed January 2026, creating an additional cost layer.
- **Enforcement actions** are intensifying: Operation Gatekeeper (December 2025) disrupted $160 million in illicit chip exports; Cadence Design Systems paid a $95 million penalty (July 2025).
- **Extraterritorial scope** now covers U.S. persons providing support for AI model training for Chinese entities, and IaaS providers training models on behalf of Chinese customers.

The paradox of export controls: each tightening strengthens the political case for China's self-sufficiency investment, accelerates domestic substitution, and creates a larger addressable market for Chinese chip companies — even if those chips are inferior on a per-unit basis. The $98 billion in combined government and corporate semiconductor spending in 2025 is a direct response to U.S. restrictions.

## 4. Risk Factors

| Risk | Severity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Semiconductor yield/capacity constraints | **High** | SMIC's 20% yield at 7nm vs. TSMC's 60%+ is a fundamental cost penalty. SMIC capacity is shared across too many customers. |
| U.S. export control escalation | **High** | Further restrictions on DUV equipment, EDA software, or HBM memory could materially slow progress. |
| Overcrowding in foundation models | **Medium** | Rapid commoditization of LLMs may compress margins before monetization paths mature. |
| Data governance and censorship friction | **Medium** | Content moderation requirements create feature gaps vs. Western models in global markets. Western adoption of Chinese AI remains limited. |
| Geopolitical decoupling | **Medium** | Accelerating tech bifurcation could lock Chinese AI companies out of developed-market revenue pools. |
| Capital misallocation | **Medium** | Massive state-directed spending carries the risk of overcapacity and poor returns, as seen in previous Chinese industrial policy cycles (solar, steel). |
| Talent constraints | **Low–Medium** | China produces large numbers of AI engineers but faces a deficit in senior research talent, partly due to emigration and academic isolation. |

## 6. Conclusion

China's path to AI self-sufficiency by 2030 is not guaranteed, but the direction of travel is unmistakable. The strategic architecture — combining top-down fiscal commitment, a rapidly maturing domestic model ecosystem, industrial deployment of embodied AI, and a massive intelligent vehicle fleet generating training data at scale — is more coherent and better-resourced than any prior Chinese industrial policy initiative.

The semiconductor bottleneck remains real and will impose a sustained cost penalty on Chinese AI relative to the TSMC-enabled Western stack. But the DeepSeek moment demonstrated that algorithmic efficiency can partially offset hardware constraints, and the sheer scale of China's domestic market provides deployment advantages that no other country can replicate.

For investors, the framework is straightforward: the infrastructure layer (chips, compute, data) carries the highest conviction but the most binary risk from export controls; the application layer (intelligent vehicles, robotics, enterprise AI) offers more diversified exposure with clearer near-term revenue paths; and the platform layer (Alibaba, ByteDance, Baidu) sits in between, with the best risk-adjusted profiles for most portfolios.

The next 12–18 months will be defined by three watchpoints: SMIC yield improvement trajectory, the pace of humanoid robotics commercial deployment, and whether the U.S. further restricts DUV lithography equipment exports. These will determine whether China's 2030 AI targets represent an achievable plan or an aspirational ceiling.
