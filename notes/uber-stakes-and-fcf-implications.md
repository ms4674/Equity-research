# Uber (NYSE: UBER) — Recent Stakes & Partnerships and Implications for Free Cash Flow

_Research note — April 17, 2026_

> **Bottom line:** Uber has shifted from an asset‑light "marketplace only" stance to a hybrid model in which it underwrites the autonomous‑vehicle (AV) ecosystem with **~$10 bn of capital commitments** (≈$2.5 bn in equity stakes plus ≈$7.5 bn of fleet purchases) layered on top of a still‑rapidly‑growing core business that delivered **$9.8 bn of free cash flow (FCF) in FY2025 (+42% YoY)**. Near term (2026‑2027) the FCF impact is contained — most cash outlays are milestone‑gated, dilutive equity is largely avoided, and management has reiterated its 50% FCF return commitment. Medium term (2028‑2031), the cadence of fleet purchases and any equity true‑ups becomes the swing factor for both FCF growth and the buyback pace.

### What is genuinely _new_ vs. continuation
- **New capital intensity**: The Rivian deal and the Lucid expansion are the first time Uber has put nine‑ to ten‑figure checks behind individual AV OEMs.
- **New fleet ownership posture**: Historically Uber refused to own vehicles. It now intends to purchase robotaxi fleets (directly or via SPVs/JVs) — a structural change with balance‑sheet and depreciation implications.
- **Continuation**: The Baidu/WeRide/Wayve relationships remain primarily commercial, consistent with the "marketplace of AV providers" thesis.

## 3. How the stakes & partnerships flow through to FCF

The classic Uber FCF identity is roughly:

```
FCF ≈ (Adj. EBITDA  − cash taxes − cash interest − change in WC − cash SBC settlement)  − capex
```

Each new partnership component touches a different line:

### 3a. Equity stakes — _below the FCF line, but cash‑negative on the cash flow statement_
Equity investments in Lucid, Nuro, Rivian, and Wayve flow through **investing activities** (not operating). They therefore do **not** reduce reported FCF as Uber defines it (operating cash flow minus purchases of property and equipment). However:
- They are still **real cash out the door** (~$2.5 bn cumulatively, gated and multi‑year).
- They reduce the cash available for buybacks and pull total cash on the balance sheet down dollar‑for‑dollar at the moment of funding.
- Mark‑to‑market gains/losses on the listed positions (Lucid, Rivian) will create non‑cash P&L volatility (similar to legacy Didi/Aurora positions) but do not affect FCF.

**Implication**: On a "FCF less strategic investments" basis, 2026‑2028 FCF is materially smaller than the headline. Investors should track an **adjusted FCF after AV equity outlays** metric.

### 3b. Robotaxi fleet purchases — _the single biggest swing factor_
The ~$7.5 bn fleet portion is the line that can _actually move reported FCF_. Treatment depends on structure:
- **Owned on Uber's balance sheet**: Capex, hits FCF directly; assets depreciate over a ~4–6 year useful life. At ~$7.5 bn over roughly 2027–2031, that is ~$1.0–2.0 bn/yr of incremental capex during peak deployment years vs. a current capex run‑rate of only a few hundred million.
- **Held in JV/SPV with OEM or financial partner (e.g., PIF in the Lucid expansion)**: Off‑balance‑sheet, equity‑method losses, no direct FCF hit but Uber funds its proportionate share via investing cash flow.
- **Operating leases / fleet manager model**: Becomes an operating cash outflow (rentals embedded in COGS) — _does_ reduce FCF, but smooths the timing.

Management has signaled a **mix** — Uber is positioning as a marketplace, suggesting fleet ownership is the exception, not the rule, but the Rivian and Lucid commitments imply at least partial direct ownership during the seeding phase.

**Implication**: In a base case where fleet outlays are 60% off‑balance‑sheet / 40% on‑balance‑sheet, FCF impact is on the order of **$0.4–0.8 bn/yr in 2027‑2028** rising to **$1.0–1.5 bn/yr by 2029‑2030** — a 5‑15% drag vs. an "unaffected" trajectory.

### 3c. Operating P&L impact of partnerships
- **Take‑rate compression risk**: AV trips currently carry a different unit economic profile. Margin per AV trip is a function of (rider fare) − (per‑trip vehicle cost paid to OEM partner). Early evidence from Waymo/Phoenix suggests AV take‑rates can be _higher_ than human‑driver ride share if Uber owns the rider relationship; lower if the OEM controls pricing.
- **Insurance & operations**: Uber retains insurance and customer‑care obligations on AV trips → some incremental opex, mostly variable.
- **Marketing**: Modest uplift to seed AV demand in launch cities.

Net‑net, near‑term P&L drag from AV is limited because volumes are still rounding error; the offset is material only in 2028+.

### 3d. Working capital and tax
- No major working capital change expected; AV partnerships do not introduce structurally different payment terms.
- Cash taxes: Uber became a federal cash taxpayer in 2024–2025 as NOLs continue to be utilized; AV‑related impairments or losses on equity stakes could generate **deferred tax assets** but won't immediately shield cash.

Assumptions: 2026 base FCF grows ~25% to ~$12.3 bn ex‑AV. Equity stake outlays of $0.8 bn in 2026, $0.6 bn in 2027, $0.4 bn in 2028 (gated). Fleet purchase capex $0.3 bn / $0.8 bn / $1.4 bn / $1.8 bn in 2026/27/28/29.

| Scenario | 2026E FCF | 2027E FCF | 2028E FCF | 2029E FCF | Cum. capital returned ('26‑'29) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Base** (mix of on/off B/S fleet, milestones met) | $12.0 bn | $13.5 bn | $14.5 bn | $16.0 bn | ~$28 bn (≥50% payout) |
| **Bull** (most fleet off‑B/S, AV unit econ accretive by '28) | $12.3 bn | $14.5 bn | $17.0 bn | $20.0 bn | ~$32 bn |
| **Bear** (fleet on‑B/S, milestone slippage delays revenue, partner equity write‑downs) | $11.5 bn | $12.0 bn | $11.5 bn | $12.0 bn | ~$22 bn (payout suspended toward 35–40%) |

These are **directional** ranges meant for framing — not a published forecast. The dispersion is dominated by (i) how fleets are financed and (ii) when AV revenue ramps offset capex.

## 5. What changes vs. the prior thesis

| Pre‑2025 thesis | Updated thesis |
|---|---|
| "Asset‑light marketplace, capex < 2% of GB" | Asset‑light **for human rides**; **capital intensive** for AV seeding through ~2030 |
| Buybacks scale linearly with FCF | Buybacks remain the priority but **compete** with $1–2 bn/yr of AV‑related cash outflows |
| Limited balance‑sheet risk | Real exposure to **OEM equity volatility** (Lucid, Rivian) and **execution risk** on milestone gates |
| AV optionality was free | AV optionality is being **paid for** — the option premium is the $10 bn commitment |

## 6. Watch items / catalysts

1. **Q1 2026 print (early May 2026):** First quarter to include accounting treatment of the Rivian initial $300 m and the Lucid top‑up. Look for:
   - Disclosure on whether fleet purchases will be classified as PP&E or held for sale / off‑B/S.
   - Update on the share‑repurchase pace given new investing outflows.
   - Any explicit FY26 FCF range.
2. **Milestone gates:** The $1.25 bn Rivian commitment and remaining Lucid tranches are deployment‑gated. Gate slippage = lower cash outflow but also lower revenue.
3. **PIF and other co‑investors:** If Uber successfully syndicates fleet financing to sovereigns (PIF in Lucid expansion is the template), the FCF drag scenario materially improves.
4. **Dividend policy details (Jan 12 2026 8‑K):** Quantification of any cash dividend would shift the buyback/dividend mix and change the 50% payout math.
5. **AV trip economics disclosures:** Per‑trip contribution profit on Waymo/Lucid/Baidu trips (when management eventually breaks it out) is the single most important KPI for the AV thesis.

## 7. Conclusion

The recent partnership cadence (Rivian + Lucid expansion in March/April 2026, on top of the 2025 Lucid/Nuro/Baidu/WeRide/Wayve announcements) represents a deliberate **option‑purchase strategy**: Uber is using ~10–15% of its expected 2026‑2029 cumulative FCF to lock in privileged supply of AVs across multiple geographies and tech stacks. The trade‑off is:

- **Near term (2026‑2027):** Modest FCF pressure (~5% drag vs. unconstrained), still leaves room for a continued "aggressive" buyback. Capital return ratio likely held at ~50% but with a slightly lower absolute dollar level than a pure organic case.
- **Medium term (2028‑2030):** Fleet purchase cadence is the swing factor. If Uber keeps fleets predominantly off its balance sheet (PIF‑style syndication), the AV business turns FCF‑accretive faster. If Uber owns a meaningful share of the fleet, expect a 10‑15% FCF drag in peak deployment years that should reverse by 2031+.
- **Long term:** Optionality is now **paid for** rather than free; the equity stakes in Lucid and Rivian also create direct mark‑to‑market exposure unrelated to Uber's own operational performance.

**Investment implication:** The strategic logic is sound and the size of the commitment (≈$10 bn) is well within Uber's FCF‑generation capacity, but the **quality of FCF deteriorates** modestly because a portion of operating cash flow is now effectively earmarked for AV buildout rather than being fully discretionary. Investors should:

1. Track an **"FCF after AV cash outlays"** metric, not just headline FCF.
2. Watch for **fleet financing structures** (off‑B/S = positive) on the next 1‑2 prints.
3. Re‑underwrite the **buyback pace** assumption to ~40–50% of headline FCF rather than the prior implicit ~60%.
4. Treat the Lucid and Rivian equity positions as **non‑core financial assets** when valuing the operating business (sum‑of‑the‑parts framing).
