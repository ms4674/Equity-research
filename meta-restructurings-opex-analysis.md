# Meta Platforms (META) -- Restructurings, Job Cuts & OpEx Impact Analysis

## Executive Summary

Since 2022, Meta has executed three major restructuring waves, cutting over 25,000 positions and incurring approximately $8.1 billion in one-time restructuring charges. The restructurings produced **~$6.0 billion in annualized SG&A savings** (comparing 2022 peak to 2024 trough), while total operating expenses continued to climb due to accelerating R&D and AI-infrastructure investment. Operating margin recovered from a trough of 24.8% in 2022 to 42.2% in 2024, and revenue per employee nearly doubled from $1.35 million to $2.55 million over the same period. A fourth restructuring wave -- potentially the largest yet -- was reported in March 2026, targeting up to 20% of the workforce (~16,000 employees) to offset surging AI capital expenditures.

---

## 1. Timeline of Major Restructurings

### Wave 1 -- November 2022 (~11,000 employees, 13% of workforce)

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Date announced | November 9, 2022 |
| Employees affected | ~11,000 (13% of workforce) |
| Rationale | Over-hiring during the pandemic; slowing ad revenue; metaverse cost overruns |
| Severance | 16 weeks base pay + 2 weeks per year of service; RSU vesting through Nov 15; 6 months health insurance |
| Restructuring charges (FY 2022) | **$4.61 billion** (predominantly Q4 2022: $4.20B) |

This was Meta's first-ever mass layoff. Headcount had ballooned from 58,604 (year-end 2020) to 86,482 (year-end 2022) -- a 47.6% increase driven by pandemic-era hiring.

### Wave 2 -- "Year of Efficiency" (March-December 2023, ~10,000 additional employees)

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Date announced | March 14, 2023 |
| Employees affected | ~10,000 laid off; ~5,000 open roles cancelled |
| Rationale | "Year of Efficiency"; flattening management layers; closing lower-priority projects |
| Restructuring charges (FY 2023) | **$3.45 billion** (facilities consolidation, severance, personnel) |
| Headcount at year-end 2023 | 67,317 (down 22.2% from 86,482 at year-end 2022) |

Zuckerberg framed 2023 as the "Year of Efficiency" -- eliminating layers of middle management, flattening reporting structures, and closing satellite offices. By year-end 2023, Meta had shed ~19,165 employees from the 2022 peak.

### Wave 3 -- Performance-Based Cuts (February 2025, ~3,600 employees)

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Date announced | January 2025; executed February 2025 |
| Employees affected | ~3,600 (approximately 5% of workforce) |
| Rationale | "Raising the bar" on performance management; clearing room for AI talent |
| Severance | 16 weeks base pay + 2 weeks per year of service |
| Hardest-hit teams | Facebook (335 cuts), Horizon VR (244), Reality Labs devices (195) |

Despite labeling these as low-performer exits, some terminated employees reported having received strong recent performance reviews, generating controversy about the true nature of the cuts.

### Wave 4 (Planned) -- March 2026 (~15,000-16,000 employees, up to 20%)

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Date reported | March 14, 2026 (Reuters exclusive) |
| Employees potentially affected | Up to ~16,000 (20% of ~79,000 workforce) |
| Rationale | Offsetting AI infrastructure spending; $115-135B capex guided for 2026 |
| Status | Executives identifying cost-saving targets; magnitude not finalized |

If executed at the upper end, this would be Meta's largest single restructuring event, surpassing the combined 2022-2023 cuts. Meta's stock rose ~3% on the reports, signaling investor support.

## 4. Quantifying OpEx Savings

### 4a. Direct SG&A Savings (Marketing & Sales + G&A)

The restructurings had the most visible impact on SG&A (selling, general & administrative) expenses.

| Metric | 2022 (Peak) | 2024 (Post-Restructuring) | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing & Sales | $15.26B | $11.35B | **-$3.91B (-25.6%)** |
| G&A | $11.82B | $9.74B | **-$2.08B (-17.6%)** |
| **Total SG&A** | **$27.08B** | **$21.09B** | **-$5.99B (-22.1%)** |

Meta achieved approximately **$6.0 billion in annualized SG&A savings** comparing the 2022 peak to the 2024 baseline, even as revenue grew 41% over the same period.

### 4b. Restructuring-Adjusted Cost Growth

Stripping out restructuring charges shows the underlying cost trajectory:

| Year | Total Costs & Expenses | Less: Restructuring | Adjusted OpEx | YoY Growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $87.67B | $4.61B | $83.06B | -- |
| 2023 | $88.15B | $3.45B | $84.70B | +2.0% |
| 2024 | $95.12B | ~$0B | $95.12B | +12.3% |
| 2025 | $117.69B | ~$0B | $117.69B | +23.7% |

Excluding one-time charges, core operating costs grew only 2.0% in 2023 despite 15.7% revenue growth -- demonstrating the leverage from the restructurings. By 2024-2025, cost growth re-accelerated as Meta poured resources into AI and compute infrastructure.

### 4c. Revenue Per Employee (Efficiency Metric)

| Year | Revenue | Headcount | Rev / Employee | YoY Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | $85.97B | 58,604 | $1.47M | -- |
| 2021 | $117.93B | 71,970 | $1.64M | +11.6% |
| 2022 | $116.61B | 86,482 | $1.35M | -17.7% |
| 2023 | $134.90B | 67,317 | $2.00M | +48.5% |
| 2024 | $164.50B | 74,067 | $2.22M | +10.9% |

Revenue per employee surged **48.5% in 2023** -- the single largest efficiency gain among Big Tech peers. By 2025, each employee generated $2.55 million in revenue, up **89% from the 2022 trough** of $1.35 million.

### 4d. Operating Cost as % of Revenue

| Year | OpEx / Revenue | SG&A / Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 62.0% | 21.1% |
| 2021 | 60.4% | 20.2% |
| 2022 | 75.2% | 23.2% |
| 2023 | 65.3% | 17.6% |

SG&A intensity halved from 23.2% of revenue in 2022 to 12.0% in 2025, representing a structurally leaner operating model. Total cost intensity at 57.8-58.6% in 2024-2025 is the lowest in Meta's recent history, despite massive AI/infrastructure investment.

## 5. Where the Savings Went: Capital Expenditure Surge

| Year | Capex | YoY Change |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | $15.12B | -- |
| 2021 | $18.57B | +22.8% |
| 2022 | $31.43B | +69.3% |
| 2023 | $27.27B | -13.2% |
| 2024 | $37.26B | +36.6% |
| 2025 | $69.69B | +87.1% |
| 2026E | $115-135B | +65-94% |

The operational savings from headcount reductions were effectively redeployed into AI infrastructure. Capex nearly quintupled from $15.1B in 2020 to $69.7B in 2025, with 2026 guidance of $115-135B representing a further near-doubling. This spending funds data center construction (targeting $600B through 2028), AI compute clusters, and custom silicon development.

The $8.1 billion in restructuring charges through 2023 can be viewed as a two-year investment that generated ~$6.0 billion in annualized SG&A savings, implying a payback period of roughly 16 months on the direct cost reductions alone.

## 7. Key Takeaways

1. **Three completed restructuring waves** (Nov 2022, 2023, Feb 2025) eliminated over 25,000 positions and incurred ~$8.1 billion in one-time charges.

2. **Annualized SG&A savings of ~$6.0 billion** were achieved by 2024 vs. the 2022 peak, with SG&A intensity falling from 23.2% to 12.0% of revenue.

3. **Total operating expenses continued to rise** ($87.7B in 2022 to $117.7B in 2025) -- but grew far slower than revenue, driving operating margin expansion from 24.8% to 42.2%.

4. **Revenue per employee nearly doubled** from $1.35M (2022) to $2.55M (2025), reflecting a structurally more efficient workforce.

5. **OpEx savings were redeployed into AI capex**, which grew from $31.4B (2022) to $69.7B (2025) with $115-135B guided for 2026.

6. **A fourth restructuring wave** (March 2026, up to ~16,000 employees) may further reduce operating costs to fund the next phase of AI infrastructure buildout.

7. **Operating margin at 41-42%** in 2024-2025 exceeds pre-restructuring levels (38-40% in 2020-2021), even as the company invests aggressively in AI -- underscoring the lasting efficiency improvements from the restructurings.
