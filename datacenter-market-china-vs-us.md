# Datacenter Market: China vs. US — Hyperscalers & Neoclouds

**Equity Research — Market Sizing & Time Series**
**May 2026**

---

## Executive Summary

The global datacenter market is undergoing a structural break driven by AI infrastructure demand. This note presents time-series data on market size and capital expenditure across two geographies (US and China) and two operator classes (hyperscalers and neoclouds). The key finding: the US leads overwhelmingly in absolute spend and is pulling further ahead via the neocloud explosion, while China is accelerating from a smaller base with a fundamentally different market structure — state-directed, telecom-heavy, and capacity-constrained by semiconductor access.

**Key observations:**
- 2022–2023 was a brief deceleration as post-COVID overbuilding unwound (Amazon cut capex 9% in 2022). The AI investment thesis had not yet translated into spend.

### 1.2 Broader Hyperscaler Universe

Including Apple, Oracle, IBM Cloud, and others, total hyperscaler capex exceeded **$500 billion in 2025** (Synergy Research / MTN Consulting). Since ChatGPT's launch, quarterly hyperscale operator capex has grown approximately 180%.

**Key observations:**

## 6. Structural Differences: Why the Markets Don't Compare Linearly

The raw numbers tell only part of the story. The US and China datacenter ecosystems differ in fundamental ways that affect how capital translates into compute capacity and, ultimately, AI capability.

### 6.1 Hardware Economics

| Factor | US | China |
|--------|-----|-------|
| Leading GPU | Nvidia H200 / B200 | Huawei Ascend 910B/C, Cambricon Siyuan 590/690 |
| Foundry access | TSMC 3nm/4nm | SMIC 7nm (N+2); ~20% yield |
| HBM supply | Full access (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron) | Restricted; dependent on Korean suppliers |
| Cost per FLOP | Benchmark | Estimated 2–4x US equivalent |
| Effective compute per $ capex | 1.0x | ~0.3–0.5x |

China spends roughly 12% as much as the US on hyperscaler capex but gets substantially less than 12% of the compute. The yield penalty at SMIC, the performance gap between Ascend 910B and Nvidia H200, and restricted HBM access mean that **China's effective AI compute capacity is likely 5–8% of the US total**, despite a capex ratio of ~12%.

### 6.2 Market Structure

| Dimension | US | China |
|-----------|-----|-------|
| Dominant operator class | Private hyperscalers + neoclouds | Private hyperscalers + state telecoms |
| Neocloud ecosystem | Deep, liquid (CoreWeave, Lambda, Crusoe) | Nascent; hardware-constrained |
| Power infrastructure | Emerging constraint (nuclear, gas, renewables) | EDWC policy directs capacity to western provinces |
| Regulatory environment | Light-touch (state-level permitting focus) | State-directed; approval-driven |
| Customer base | Global (serving worldwide demand) | Primarily domestic |

### 6.3 Growth Trajectory

The US market is in an acceleration phase driven by two reinforcing dynamics: hyperscaler capex at all-time highs *and* a neocloud layer that barely existed three years ago and is growing at 200%+ annually. CoreWeave alone has a $99 billion revenue backlog.

China's market is also accelerating, but from a smaller base and with a binding constraint — chip supply — that the US does not face. China's hyperscaler capex is growing at roughly 70% YoY (2024–2025), comparable to US growth rates, but the absolute gap is widening because both are growing at similar percentages on very different bases.

## 8. Key Takeaways

1. **The US is pulling away in absolute terms.** US hyperscaler capex reached $416 billion in 2025 vs. China's ~$51 billion — and the gap is widening, not narrowing.

2. **Neoclouds are a US phenomenon — for now.** The neocloud market barely exists in China ($0.4B) vs. the US/global market ($25B+). Hardware restrictions are the binding constraint.

3. **China compensates with state capital.** When telecom operator and government-directed infrastructure spending is included, China's total datacenter investment is larger than private-sector capex alone suggests — but still far below US levels.

4. **Compute efficiency gap matters more than capex gap.** China gets 0.3–0.5x the effective compute per dollar of capex due to inferior chip yields, performance, and memory access. The true AI compute gap is wider than the spending gap.

5. **Both markets are in structural growth.** Neither is close to saturation. US growth is driven by the AI training + inference buildout; China's growth is driven by policy-directed AI integration + self-sufficiency investment.

6. **The hardware constraint is China's defining challenge.** Until SMIC yields improve materially or a breakthrough in domestic chip design narrows the performance gap, China's datacenter buildout will convert capex into compute less efficiently than the US — effectively a structural tax on every AI workload.
