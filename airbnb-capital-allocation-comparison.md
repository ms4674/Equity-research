## 1. Airbnb Debt Issuance and Use of Proceeds

### Overview of Outstanding Debt

Airbnb's debt profile is notably simple. The company has a single major debt instrument: **$2.0 billion of 0% convertible senior notes due March 15, 2026**, issued in March 2021. As of year-end 2024, long-term debt stood at approximately **$1,995 million** (net of issuance costs), with no current portion of long-term debt coming due within the year.

### Use of Proceeds

Airbnb deployed the net proceeds from the 2021 convertible offering as follows:

- **$100.2 million** was used to fund capped call transactions, which were designed to reduce potential dilution upon conversion of the notes.
- **~$1,879 million** (the remainder) was combined with existing cash to **repay outstanding indebtedness**, including pre-IPO term loans and other legacy obligations incurred during the pandemic period.

The capital raise was strategically timed: in early 2021, Airbnb was just months post-IPO (December 2020) and carried pandemic-era debt at significantly higher interest rates. By issuing zero-coupon convertible notes, Airbnb effectively refinanced expensive debt with zero-cost capital, dramatically reducing its interest burden.

### March 2026 Maturity: Current Situation

The convertible notes mature on **March 15, 2026** -- just days from now. A critical observation:

- Airbnb's stock price is trading around **~$130/share** (based on recent insider transaction filings), which is **well below the $288.64 conversion price**.
- The notes are therefore **significantly out of the money**, meaning holders have no economic incentive to convert. At maturity, Airbnb will owe the **full $2.0 billion in cash**.
- With **$11.0 billion in corporate cash and investments** on the balance sheet (as of Q4 2025), Airbnb can comfortably retire this debt from existing liquidity without needing to refinance.

This forthcoming $2 billion cash outflow will reduce Airbnb's cash hoard but still leave the company with approximately **$9 billion** in liquidity, maintaining a strong financial position. Notably, Airbnb has not announced any new debt issuance to refinance or replace the maturing convertible notes, suggesting management intends to retire the notes entirely from cash on hand.

Despite the high gross debt figure, its enormous free cash flow generation keeps net debt manageable at under $1 billion. Booking has strategically used the debt markets to fund its massive shareholder return programs while maintaining investment-grade credit ratings.

**Expedia** sits in the middle, with ~$4.5 billion in long-term debt that has been declining (down from $6.3 billion in 2023). The company issued $1 billion in 5.400% senior notes due 2035 in February 2025, using proceeds for general corporate purposes including debt repayment and shareholder returns. Expedia's declining debt trajectory signals a preference for deleveraging.

As a more mature company with lower organic growth opportunities relative to its cash flow, Booking uses buybacks as its dominant return mechanism, consistently reducing float.

## 3. Key Differences and Strategic Implications

### Airbnb: Growth-Stage Capital Allocation in a Mature Body

Airbnb's capital allocation is distinctive in several respects:

1. **Near-zero leverage post March 2026.** Upon retiring the convertible notes, Airbnb could become effectively debt-free, a stark contrast to peers. This provides maximum financial flexibility but also raises capital efficiency questions -- with $9 billion in excess cash earning below the company's cost of equity, there is an argument for more aggressive capital deployment.

2. **No dividend, maximum buyback intensity.** By allocating ~31% of revenue to repurchases, Airbnb is the most aggressive buyer of its own stock as a percentage of its economic output. The absence of a dividend keeps capital return fully discretionary and flexible.

3. **Organic over inorganic investment.** With only one acquisition in five years as a public company, Airbnb bets on internal innovation (AI, platform redesign) rather than M&A. This minimizes integration risk but could limit the pace of vertical expansion.

4. **Potential for future debt issuance.** With no debt post-maturity and strong investment-grade-quality financials ($4.6 billion FCF, $9 billion net cash), Airbnb has significant untapped debt capacity. A future issuance could fund accelerated M&A, supplemental buybacks, or a dividend initiation -- though management has given no signals of this intent.

### Booking Holdings: Mature Compounder with Maximum Shareholder Returns

1. **Leveraged return model.** Booking uses its $16.9 billion debt stack to amplify shareholder returns, spending more on buybacks and dividends than it generates in free cash flow in some years. The diversified maturity profile (out to 2046) provides stable, long-term financing.

2. **Dual-channel returns.** With both buybacks and dividends, Booking appeals to a broader investor base. The $20 billion buyback authorization is one of the largest in the travel sector.

3. **Strategic reinvestment at scale.** The $700 million incremental investment in 2026 (funded by a $500-550 million transformation savings program) demonstrates a "self-funding" approach to growth investment, effectively using efficiency gains to pay for new initiatives.

### Expedia: Deleveraging Turnaround with Balanced Returns

1. **Active debt management.** Expedia is the only one of the three actively deleveraging (from $6.3 billion to $4.5 billion in two years) while simultaneously issuing new debt at longer maturities to extend the maturity profile. The February 2025 issuance of $1 billion in 10-year notes at 5.4% refinances nearer-term obligations.

2. **Most balanced allocation.** Expedia distributes capital across debt reduction, buybacks, dividends, and acquisitions in more balanced proportions than either peer. This reflects its position as an operational turnaround story where financial stability is a priority alongside shareholder returns.

3. **B2B as a growth engine.** Unlike Airbnb and Booking, Expedia is investing heavily in its B2B channel (26% bookings growth), which could eventually command a premium valuation multiple if it becomes a material contributor to earnings.

## 5. Investment Considerations

### For Airbnb
- The March 2026 note maturity is a non-event given liquidity levels, but the resulting debt-free balance sheet raises the question of optimal capital structure. Management may face growing pressure to either (a) initiate a modest dividend to attract income-oriented investors, or (b) lever up the balance sheet to enhance ROE and fund larger-scale M&A.
- The 0% coupon on the maturing notes was an extraordinary financing achievement; any future debt issuance will carry meaningful interest costs in the current rate environment.
- At ~$130/share (vs. ~$180 at IPO and ~$200+ peak), the aggressive buyback posture suggests management sees significant share price dislocation relative to intrinsic value.

### For Booking Holdings
- The company's ability to sustain $7+ billion annual buybacks depends on continued FCF growth; any macro-driven travel downturn would pressure this model given the high gross debt load.
- The $20 billion buyback authorization represents nearly 8% of the company's current market cap, signaling multi-year commitment to share count reduction.

### For Expedia
- Expedia's deleveraging and improving FCF profile are positive, but the company needs to demonstrate that its operational turnaround can translate into sustainable margin expansion to justify accelerating capital returns.
- The 5.4% coupon on the 2025 senior notes issuance highlights the higher cost of capital Expedia faces relative to its better-positioned peers.
