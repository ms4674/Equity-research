# Terafab: Project Details, Funding, and Scope

## Executive Summary

Terafab is a planned semiconductor fabrication plant jointly developed by **Tesla**, **SpaceX**, and **xAI**. Announced by Elon Musk on March 21, 2026, during an event at the Seaholm Power Plant in Austin, Texas, the project aims to build the world's largest vertically integrated chip production facility. Total funding raised stands at approximately **$25 billion**, with the facility targeting **2-nanometer process technology** and an annual output exceeding **one terawatt of AI compute capacity**.

## Funding and Capital Structure

### Total Investment

The Terafab project carries an estimated total cost of **$20–25 billion**, with $25 billion reportedly raised as of early 2026. The capital is sourced across the three participating entities—Tesla, SpaceX, and xAI—reflecting a shared strategic interest in securing proprietary semiconductor supply.

### Justification for Scale

Musk stated that current global fab output covers only approximately **2% of what Tesla, SpaceX, and xAI need combined**, framing the project as a necessity driven by supply constraints rather than speculative ambition.

### Complementary Supply Agreement

In parallel with the Terafab buildout, Tesla has secured a **$16.5 billion AI chip supply agreement with Samsung Electronics** at Samsung's Taylor, Texas facility (located approximately 40 minutes from the Terafab site). This dual-track approach provides near-term chip supply while the Terafab facility scales to production volumes.

---

## Scope and Vertical Integration

Terafab is designed as a **fully vertically integrated** semiconductor facility, consolidating the entire production process under one roof:

1. **Chip Design** — In-house architecture for AI inference and compute chips
2. **Lithography** — Advanced patterning at 2 nm node
3. **Fabrication** — Wafer processing and transistor manufacturing
4. **Memory Production** — On-site memory fabrication for tightly coupled compute-memory architectures
5. **Advanced Packaging** — Chiplet assembly, 3D stacking, and heterogeneous integration
6. **Testing** — Quality assurance and binning

### Production Mix

The facility plans a dual production model:

- **~20%** — Terrestrial AI edge inference chips (AI5/AI6 generation) for Tesla vehicles and Optimus robots
- **~80%** — Radiation-hardened, space-qualified AI chips (D3 generation) for SpaceX orbital AI infrastructure

## Competitive and Strategic Context

### Industry Positioning

At full scale, Terafab's output of 1 million wafer starts per month would represent roughly **70% of TSMC's current global output**, positioning it as a direct challenge to the established foundry leaders. Currently, only **TSMC and Samsung** operate at the 2 nm process node.

### Strategic Rationale

The project represents a fundamental strategic shift for Musk's companies—moving from dependence on third-party foundries (primarily TSMC) toward **semiconductor self-sufficiency**. The rationale rests on three pillars:

1. **Supply Security** — Reducing geopolitical risk from concentration of advanced chip manufacturing in Taiwan and South Korea
2. **Cost Control** — Eliminating foundry margins on high-volume, proprietary chip designs
3. **Speed of Iteration** — Enabling tighter design-to-fabrication feedback loops for AI chip development

### Terafab in Context

At its **initial capacity of 100,000 WSPM** at the 2 nm node, Terafab would immediately become the **second-largest source of 2 nm wafers globally**, behind only TSMC's combined 2 nm output. It would exceed Samsung's entire 2 nm ramp and match Intel's 18A output across all of its Arizona fabs.

At **full scale of 1,000,000 WSPM**, Terafab would rival TSMC's entire installed capacity across all nodes and represent approximately **77% of TSMC's current total output** — an unprecedented concentration of cutting-edge capacity in a single facility operated by a non-traditional semiconductor company.

## Investment Implications and Risk Factors

### Bull Case

- Resolves anticipated chip supply constraints for autonomous driving, robotics, and AI workloads
- Vertical integration creates structural cost advantages at scale
- Positions Tesla/SpaceX/xAI ahead of competitors in securing dedicated compute capacity
- Analyst Dan Ives (Wedbush) maintains "Outperform" on Tesla with $600 price target, citing Terafab as critical infrastructure

### Bear Case / Risk Factors

- **Capital Intensity** — $20–25B investment with long ramp-up periods before revenue contribution
- **Execution Risk** — 2 nm yield challenges are significant; no greenfield fab has achieved competitive yields at this node without years of process development
- **Scaling Hurdles** — Ramping from 100K to 1M wafer starts per month requires solving logistics, equipment procurement, and workforce challenges simultaneously
- **Regulatory Risk** — NHTSA's ongoing FSD investigation and potential policy changes could affect demand drivers
- **Capital Raise Risk** — A potential equity offering may be required, introducing dilution risk for existing Tesla shareholders
- **Valuation Concerns** — Tesla trades at ~336x P/E, already reflecting significant optionality from AI and autonomy; limited near-term upside from the Terafab narrative alone
- **AI6 Delays** — The next-generation AI6 chip has reportedly been pushed to late 2027, constraining the technology roadmap

### Stocks with Exposure

Companies most directly exposed to Terafab developments:

- **Tesla (TSLA)** — Primary equity vehicle; direct beneficiary and capital provider
- **Samsung Electronics** — Supply agreement partner and potential process technology licensor
- **Equipment Suppliers** — ASML, Applied Materials, Lam Research, and Tokyo Electron as key enablers of 2 nm fabrication equipment
