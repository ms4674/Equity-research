# Equity-research

## Revenue exposure to teens under 16 — Meta, Google, Snap, Pinterest

`Under16_Revenue_Exposure_Model.xlsx` is a transparent, source-anchored model that estimates
the share of each platform's advertising revenue attributable to **users under age 16**
(primarily 13–15 year-olds, plus the under-13s present despite 13+ minimum-age rules), and
quantifies the **growth headwind from under-16 social-media bans** (Australia + the global pipeline).

It is generated by `build_model.py` (run `python3 build_model.py` to rebuild). The model is now
based on **FY2025 actuals** (FY2024 retained for the growth comparison on the *Financials* tab).

### Headline base-case estimate (FY2025)

| Company | FY2025 revenue base | Under-16 share (blended) | Under-16 revenue (base) | Low – High |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Meta (Facebook + Instagram) | $196.2bn ad rev | ~5.9% | **~$11.7bn** | $7.6–15.7bn |
| Google / Alphabet (YouTube + Search + Network) | $294.7bn ad rev | ~4.4% | **~$12.8bn** | $8.3–17.3bn |
| Snap (Snapchat) | $5.9bn rev | ~25.9% | **~$1.5bn** | $1.0–2.1bn |
| Pinterest | $4.2bn rev | ~2.5% | **~$0.1bn** | $0.07–0.14bn |
| **Total (4 companies)** | **~$501bn** | **~5.2%** | **~$26.1bn** | **$17.0–35.3bn** |

FY2025 revenue growth for context: **Meta +22%, Alphabet +15%, Snap +11%, Pinterest +16%.**

Snap has by far the highest *relative* exposure (a teen-core app); Pinterest the lowest
(older, female-skewed audience with personalised-ad restrictions for under-18s). In absolute
dollars, YouTube and Instagram dominate.

### Regulatory headwind — under-16 bans (new `Regulatory Headwind` tab)

Australia's under-16 ban took effect **10 December 2025**. It covers **Facebook, Instagram,
Snapchat and YouTube** but **excludes Pinterest, Messenger, WhatsApp and YouTube Kids**. The
estimated *at-risk* revenue (the under-16 slice a ban removes, after enforcement leakage):

| Scenario | Meta | Google | Snap | Pinterest | Total/yr |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| **A — Australia (in force)** | ~$120m (≈6bp) | ~$97m (≈2bp) | ~$22m (≈37bp) | $0 (excluded) | ~$0.24bn |
| **B — + UK & EU pipeline (~2026–27)** | ~$758m | ~$580m | ~$87m | ~$5m | ~$1.43bn |
| **C — + US states (broad adoption)** | ~$819m (≈41bp) | ~$624m (≈15bp) | ~$97m (≈164bp) | ~$5m (≈12bp) | ~$1.55bn |

*bp = basis points of FY2025 revenue (Google measured vs Alphabet total; YouTube-only exposure).*

**Takeaways:** the direct revenue hit is small for the giants and **zero for Pinterest**; it is
most material for **Snap**. The larger risks are regulatory **contagion** (40+ countries now
active — Denmark, France, Greece, Portugal, Spain, Norway, UK, US states) and erosion of the
youth **user funnel** (a multi-year drag on the future-ARPU pipeline), neither of which is fully
captured in the one-year revenue figures above.

> These are **modelled estimates**, not disclosed figures. No platform reports revenue by user
> age, and issuers caution that age data is unreliable because under-age users mis-state their
> age (Meta 10-K; Ofcom: 40% of 8–17s admit giving a fake age). Actual exposure may be higher.

### Method (see the workbook's *Methodology* tab)

1. **Anchor** — US under-18 ad-revenue *shares* from the Harvard / PLOS ONE 2023 simulation
   study (Instagram 16%, Snapchat 41.4%, YouTube 27%, Facebook 1.9%).
2. **Under-16 split** — keep all under-13 revenue plus 57% of the 13–17 band (3 of 5 single-year
   ages, mild older-teen engagement skew).
3. **Regionalise** — scale the US share by regional multipliers (US/NA 1.00, Europe 0.95,
   APAC 1.05, Rest of World 1.15) and apply to disclosed FY2024 regional revenue.
4. **Surfaces** — Meta blended across Instagram/Facebook/Messenger; Google split into
   YouTube/Search/Network; Snap and Pinterest single-surface.
5. **Scenarios** — Low/Base/High scalars (0.65/1.00/1.35) bracket model uncertainty.
