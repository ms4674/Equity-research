# 2Q 2026 Earnings Preview — Digital Advertising / Social Media

**Coverage:** Alphabet (GOOGL), Meta Platforms (META), Snap (SNAP), Pinterest (PINS), Reddit (RDDT)
**Date of preview:** July 10, 2026 (prices/estimates as of July 9–10, 2026)

## Setup: the quarter in one page

Q2 2026 earnings season kicks off the week of July 14 against a complicated macro backdrop (May CPI re-accelerated to 4.2%, a weak June jobs report, and continued tariff uncertainty), but the digital-ad group enters the season with sector tech estimates having been **revised up** — consensus models ~+17% EPS growth for the tech sector overall. Within the group:

- **The bar is highest where consensus sits above management guidance.** Reddit's Street revenue number (~$729M) is *above* the top of its own $715–725M guide, and Meta's (~$60.2–61.3B) is at/above the top of its $58–61B guide. Both names need beat-and-raise quarters just to meet expectations.
- **Snap reports first (July 16) and is the clearest negative-revision story**, with sell-side price-target cuts landing the week before the print (Goldman $7→$6, Wells Fargo $7→$5) and consensus revenue (~$1.57B) still sitting *above* the $1.52–1.55B guided range that already embeds the loss of Perplexity revenue and Middle East headwinds.
- **Alphabet and Pinterest carry the cleanest upward-revision trajectories** following blowout Q1 prints, though both have seen a slight negative drift in the last 30 days as analysts fine-tune.
- **Common threads to watch:** (1) AI capex vs. monetization — the four hyperscalers have spent >$220B on AI capex over four quarters and investors want revenue proof, not just depreciation; (2) AI Overviews / LLM answer engines re-routing traffic (risk for Reddit, debate for Google Search); (3) KIDS Act progress in the Senate as a sector-wide regulatory overhang; (4) FX, a ~1–2pt revenue tailwind for most names this quarter.

**Report dates:** SNAP Jul 16 → GOOGL Jul 22 → META Jul 29 → RDDT Jul 29–30 → PINS early August (est.).

## Alphabet (GOOGL) — July 22, after close

**Consensus (Q2'26):** Revenue ~$116.8B (+21% YoY; range $113.6–120.1B, 37 analysts); EPS ~$2.86–2.89 (+~24% YoY vs. $2.31; range $2.59–3.60, 42 analysts). FY26 EPS ~$14.2–14.3 on ~$488B revenue.

**Estimate revisions:** The dominant feature is the **post-Q1 step-change**: FY26 EPS consensus jumped from $11.47 (90 days ago) to $14.22 today (+24%) after Q1 EPS of $5.11 crushed the $2.67 consensus (+92% surprise, flattered by one-time items). Q2 EPS has crept up from $2.76 to $2.89 (+4.7%) over 90 days. Near-term drift is mildly negative — over the last 30 days, 2 analysts raised and 4 cut Q2 numbers — but the full-year number has been stable-to-higher (7 up vs. 4 down revisions in 30 days). Alphabet has beaten EPS consensus in 13 of the last 15 quarters (median beat ~7.5%); revenue beats ran +5.6% to +9.7% over the last four prints.

**Sources of upside:**
- **Google Cloud** is the single biggest swing factor: Q1 grew 63% and crossed $20B/quarter, well above consensus. Backlog and capacity commentary can extend the story.
- **Search resilience + AI monetization:** evidence that AI Overviews and Gemini integrations lift ad conversions/yields would defend the multiple; Q1 showed no cannibalization of paid clicks.
- Continued **YouTube** strength and cost discipline supporting double-digit operating income growth.

**Sources of disappointment:**
- **Capex escalation and financing:** the stock already eased after Alphabet raised equity to fund AI expansion; another capex guide-up without matching revenue proof would pressure shares. Rising depreciation is a growing EPS headwind.
- **AI Overviews click-through economics:** any sign paid click growth is decelerating as AI answers absorb queries.
- Antitrust/remedies headlines remain a persistent tail risk.
- **Positioning:** the stock is up ~98% over 12 months ($358.89); with 43 of 53 analysts at Strong Buy, a merely in-line quarter may not be enough.

**Net setup:** Positive revisions, strong beat history, but a crowded long after a near-double in the stock. Upside case rests on Cloud acceleration + AI-ad proof points; risk is capex fatigue.

## Meta Platforms (META) — July 29, after close

**Consensus (Q2'26):** Revenue ~$60.2–61.3B vs. guidance of $58–61B (i.e., consensus at/above the top of the guided range), +~27–29% YoY; EPS ~$7.17–7.32 (vs. $7.14 LY — roughly flat YoY optically, but LY included favorable items; Q1'26 EPS of $10.44 included a large tax benefit). FY26 EPS ~$30–32 on ~$253B revenue.

**Estimate revisions:** Q2 revenue consensus has been **revised up ~1.1% over the past 3 months**, and FY26 revenue estimates (~$252.9B, +26% YoY) have drifted higher post-Q1. The offset: FY26 **EPS growth is compressed (~+9%)** and consensus free cash flow for FY26 is approximately **zero/negative (~-$0.3B)** because of the capex guide-up to **$125–145B** (raised from $120–135B in April, alongside a $107B step-up in contractual commitments). Meta has beaten EPS consensus in every recent quarter (last four surprises: +$1.39, +$0.51, +$0.72, +$3.77).

**Sources of upside:**
- **Ad pricing and AI-driven targeting** (Advantage+ / automated campaigns) drove Q1's +33% growth; checks suggest continued strength, and Q2 guidance embeds a ~2% FX tailwind.
- **Beat-and-raise mechanics:** guidance ranges have proven conservative; the Q3 revenue guide matters more than the Q2 actuals for the stock reaction.
- Cost discipline: full-year expense guidance ($162–169B) was left unchanged even as capex rose; May headcount reduction supports opex leverage.

**Sources of disappointment:**
- **Capex/depreciation spiral:** another capex guide-up without explicit ROI evidence is the key bear catalyst — Q1's raise initially sent shares down. Watch depreciation commentary and any FY27 capex framing.
- **Consensus above the top of guidance** leaves no room for an in-line quarter.
- EU regulatory friction (data privacy, AI content rules) as a drag on European ad revenue; KIDS Act headlines.
- Margin watch: whether operating margin holds ~40% as infrastructure costs ramp.

**Net setup:** Strong momentum name (stock +4.7% on July 9 to $631.48; avg PT ~$827) with upward revenue revisions but a demanding bar: consensus above guidance, flat YoY EPS optics, and an FCF-breakeven year. The Q3 guide and capex language will decide the print.

## Snap (SNAP) — July 16, after close (first of the group)

**Consensus (Q2'26):** Revenue ~$1.57B vs. **guidance of only $1.52–1.55B** — consensus sits *above* the high end of the guided range, which itself assumes zero Perplexity contribution and persistent Middle East headwinds. Adjusted EBITDA guided to $175–200M. EPS estimates are split by convention: ~+$0.08 adjusted / ~-$0.12 GAAP (management framed ~$0.09). FY26: ~$6.7B revenue (+13%), adj. EPS ~$0.60.

**Estimate revisions:** Two-sided and deteriorating at the margin. Post-Q1, 8 analysts revised earnings **up** (cost-cut driven: >$500M annualized cost reduction planned for H2'26, $95–130M restructuring charges mostly hitting Q2). But in the week before the print, the sell side has been cutting targets: **Goldman $7→$6 (Jul 8), Wells Fargo $7→$5 (Jul 7), D.A. Davidson initiated at Hold/$5 (Jul 7)**. Consensus rating is Hold (30 of 43 at Hold) — the Street is on the sidelines. Q1 revenue was only in line (~$1.53B, +12%) though EBITDA beat by ~9%.

**Sources of upside:**
- **Cost structure / EBITDA:** Q1 EBITDA more than doubled YoY and beat by 8.6%; restructuring benefits begin flowing in Q2–Q3. An EBITDA beat above the $200M high end is the most achievable positive surprise.
- **Snapchat+ subscriptions** compounding >30% — the highest-quality revenue line.
- Any stabilization/de-escalation in Middle East brand spend (guidance assumed the headwind persists) or an upside surprise on DR ad platform improvements.

**Sources of disappointment:**
- **The consensus-vs-guidance gap:** printing within the guided range ($1.52–1.55B) would still be a miss vs. the ~$1.57B Street number.
- **Perplexity revenue removal** — a high-margin contribution previously expected in 2026 is now zero.
- Continued share loss of brand budgets to Meta/TikTok; geopolitical sensitivity in the Middle East region explicitly flagged by management as uncertain.
- Restructuring charges make GAAP optics ugly in Q2 specifically.

**Net setup:** The weakest revision profile and the lowest expectations in the group (stock at $4.70, Hold-rated) — but also the most obvious mechanical risk of a "miss" given consensus sits above guidance. Upside likely has to come from EBITDA/cost execution, not revenue.

## Pinterest (PINS) — early August (est.)

**Consensus (Q2'26):** Revenue ~$1.15B (+15% YoY; range $1.14–1.16B, 30 analysts) vs. guidance of $1.133–1.153B (+14–16%, incl. ~1pt FX tailwind) — consensus at the upper half of the guide. Adjusted EBITDA guided $256–276M (Street was at ~$261M when guided). EPS ~$0.36 adj. (+~9% YoY; range $0.31–0.42). FY26: ~$4.87B revenue (+15%), EPS ~$1.90.

**Estimate revisions:** Solidly positive over the quarter: Q2 EPS +16% over 90 days ($0.31 → $0.36) and FY26 EPS +10% ($1.72 → $1.90) after the Q1 blowout (revenue $1.008B vs. $966M consensus, +18% YoY; EPS $0.27 vs. $0.23; record 631M MAUs +11%; stock +17% on the print). Last-30-day drift is fractionally negative (1 down, 0 up on Q2) but numbers are essentially stable. Caution flag: PINS **missed** adjusted EPS in Q2'25 (-6%) and Q3'25 (-9%) — its bottom-line beat record is the weakest of the group, and mid-year quarters were the culprits last year.

**Sources of upside:**
- **AI-driven search/recommendations and Performance+** adoption lifting ad relevance and ARPU (Q1 ARPU +6%).
- **Third-party demand** (Google, Magnite partnerships) monetizing international users — the largest untapped ARPU pool.
- Margin leverage from the January restructuring (~15% workforce cut); guidance already reflected known tariff/consumer noise per the CFO.

**Sources of disappointment:**
- **Shopping-ad sensitivity to tariffs/consumer softness** — Pinterest skews toward retail/e-commerce budgets, and Asia-based e-commerce advertisers have been a swing factor before.
- Mature US MAU base means growth is increasingly ARPU-dependent; any international ARPU stall hurts the model.
- GAAP optics (Q1 was a GAAP loss on restructuring) and EBITDA margin vs. rising AI infrastructure costs.

**Net setup:** Constructive: positive revisions, achievable guidance, low-drama expectations, and self-help margin story — but the seasonal pattern of small mid-year EPS misses and retail-ad macro sensitivity are the watch items. Stock at $22.70 remains inexpensive relative to the group's growth.

## Reddit (RDDT) — July 29–30 (est.)

**Consensus (Q2'26):** Revenue ~$729M (+46% YoY; range $720–783M, 26 analysts) vs. **guidance of $715–725M** — consensus is *above* the top of the guide (some services show $733–743M). Adjusted EBITDA guided $285–295M. EPS ~$0.96 (vs. $0.45 LY, +113%; range $0.80–1.26). FY26: ~$3.23B revenue (+47%), EPS ~$4.97.

**Estimate revisions:** The steepest upward revisions in the group: Q2 EPS +16% over 60 days ($0.83 → $0.96) and FY26 EPS +21% ($4.12 → $4.97), all following the Q1 blowout (revenue $663M vs. ~$610M consensus, +69% YoY; ads +74%; EPS $1.01 vs. $0.56–0.58; 40% EBITDA margin; FCF $311M). Reddit has beaten EPS consensus by +138%, +54%, +33%, and +79% over the last four quarters — the Street systematically under-models it, and consensus sitting above guidance shows the market now assumes that continues.

**Sources of upside:**
- **Advertising momentum:** impressions + pricing + full-funnel demand drove 74% ad growth in Q1; international ARPU is still a fraction of US levels.
- **AI data-licensing renewals — the 2026 catalyst:** the Google (~$60M/yr) and OpenAI (~$70M/yr) deals signed in 2024 reprice this year; Wells Fargo models a combined renewal near **$550M/yr (~4x current run-rate)** at 90%+ gross margin. Any renewal announcement or bullish framing on the call is a major positive.
- Reddit is the most-cited domain in Google AI Overviews/Perplexity, strengthening its negotiating leverage.

**Sources of disappointment:**
- **The Google-dependency paradox:** organic search drives ~60% of Reddit's traffic, and AI answer engines synthesizing Reddit content without sending the click are a structural user-growth headwind (Wells Fargo pegs a ~$16B EV overhang). **Watch the US DAU line first** — Q1 DAUq was 126.8M (+17%) with international (+26%) doing the work; another quarter of US deceleration would hit the multiple hardest.
- **Licensing renewal at modest terms** (staying near ~$130M/yr) would invalidate the primary bull catalyst; RBC has flagged 5–8x fee increases as unlikely.
- Competition (Meta's Forum app launch in May knocked RDDT -6% in a day), KIDS Act exposure, and valuation (~50x earnings; stock has round-tripped from -40% YTD lows near $167 to $200.31).
- Consensus above the top of guidance means an in-line quarter is effectively a miss.

**Net setup:** Best fundamental momentum and revision trajectory in the group, with a genuine call-option on licensing renewals — but the highest expectations bar, the most volatile stock, and a live structural debate on traffic. The print is likely decided by US DAUs and licensing commentary more than the revenue number itself.
