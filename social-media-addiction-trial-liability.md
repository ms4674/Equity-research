# Social Media Addiction Trials: Meta & Google Potential Liability and Fines

## Executive Summary

Meta and Google face an unprecedented wave of litigation over allegations that their platforms were deliberately designed to addict children, causing mental health harm. As of March 2026, two landmark jury verdicts have gone against Meta — a $6 million addiction trial verdict (shared with Google) and a $375 million child exploitation verdict in New Mexico. Meta has disclosed "tens of billions" in potential litigation exposure in SEC filings, with theoretical aggregate damages across all proceedings reaching "hundreds of billions of dollars." Over 2,400 federal cases are pending in MDL 3047, with bellwether trials scheduled through 2027. EU regulatory fines of up to 6% of global revenue add further exposure.

## Part 1: Landmark Jury Verdicts (March 2026)

### 1A. KGM v. Meta — Social Media Addiction Bellwether (California State Court)

**Verdict Date:** March 25, 2026

**Court:** Los Angeles County Superior Court

**Plaintiff:** 20-year-old woman identified as "K.G.M." (called "Kaley" by her attorneys), who began using YouTube at age 6 and Instagram at age 9

**Defendants at Trial:** Meta (Instagram) and Google (YouTube). TikTok and Snapchat settled before trial under undisclosed terms.

| Category | Amount |
|----------|--------|
| Compensatory damages | $3,000,000 |
| Punitive damages | $3,000,000 |
| **Total award** | **$6,000,000** |

**Liability Split:**
| Company | Share | Total Liability |
|---------|-------|-----------------|
| Meta (Instagram) | 70% | $4,200,000 |
| Google (YouTube) | 30% | $1,800,000 |

**Key Jury Findings (10-2 vote):**
- Both companies were **negligent** in the design and operation of their platforms
- Both companies **knew or should have known** their platform designs were "dangerous"
- Both **failed to warn** users of foreseeable addiction and mental health risks
- Both acted with **"malice, oppression, or fraud"** (triggering punitive damages)
- Platform architecture itself — not just user content — creates legal liability for psychological injury

**Significance:** This is the first time a U.S. jury treated social media apps as **defective products** for being engineered to exploit developing brains. Courts rejected Big Tech's Section 230 defenses, allowing negligence and failure-to-warn claims to proceed under product liability principles.

**Pre-Trial Settlements:**
- **TikTok** settled January 27, 2026 (undisclosed terms)
- **Snapchat** settled January 20, 2026 (undisclosed terms)

**Appeals:** Both Meta and Google have stated they disagree with the verdict and plan to appeal.

### 1B. New Mexico v. Meta — Child Exploitation & Consumer Protection

**Verdict Date:** March 24, 2026

**Court:** Santa Fe, New Mexico (State District Court)

**Plaintiff:** State of New Mexico, brought by Attorney General Raúl Torrez

| Category | Amount |
|----------|--------|
| Civil penalties (Phase 1) | $375,000,000 |
| Phase 2 damages (May 2026) | TBD |

**Key Jury Findings (after 7-week trial):**
- Meta made **false or misleading statements** about platform safety
- Meta engaged in **"unconscionable" trade practices** exploiting children's vulnerabilities
- Meta violated New Mexico's Unfair Practices Act
- Thousands of individual violations found, at up to **$5,000 per violation**
- Based on approximately **37,500 affected New Mexico users**

**Background:** The case was based on a 2023 undercover investigation where state agents created decoy accounts posing as minors under 14 and documented sexual solicitations and child exploitation attempts on Instagram and Facebook.

**Second Trial Phase (May 4, 2026):**
A judge-only proceeding will determine:
- Whether Meta created a **public nuisance**
- Additional financial penalties
- Court-ordered platform changes including:
  - Mandatory age verification
  - Removal of predators
  - Restrictions on encrypted communications that shield bad actors
  - Algorithm modifications

**Note:** New Mexico sought over $2 billion; the jury awarded approximately one-fifth of that amount. Meta plans to appeal.

3. **Expert testimony admitted:** Testimony on addiction, product design, and algorithmic harm approved — strengthening plaintiffs' ability to prove causation

### Current Liability

|--------|----------------|--------|
| KGM addiction verdict | $1.8M (Google's 30% share) | Verdict March 2026; appeal pending |
| MDL 3047 (federal cases) | Undisclosed (named defendant in 2,407 cases) | Bellwether trials Jun/Aug 2026 |
| EU DSA enforcement | Up to 6% of global revenue | Investigation ongoing |

Google/Alphabet has not publicly disclosed a specific dollar estimate of its litigation exposure from social media addiction claims in the same manner as Meta. However, as a named defendant in all 2,407 MDL cases and having been found liable in the bellwether trial, exposure is significant.

### Key Differences from Meta
- YouTube was assigned **30% liability** vs. Meta's 70% in the KGM case, reflecting the jury's view that Instagram's design was more culpable
- YouTube's primary addiction concern is **autoplay and algorithmic video recommendations**, whereas Meta faces broader claims around body image (filters), social comparison, and notification loops
- Google has not been named in the New Mexico child exploitation case (that was Meta-specific)

## Part 8: Investment Implications

### Bear Case
- Verdicts establish precedent that platform design = product liability, opening floodgates for thousands of similar claims
- Meta's own disclosure of "tens of billions" in exposure suggests material financial impact
- EU DSA fines could add billions more
- Regulatory momentum (KOSA, European bans) may force costly platform redesigns
- Section 230 shield significantly weakened for design-based claims

### Bull Case
- Both verdicts will be appealed; outcomes uncertain
- KGM damages ($6M) are modest relative to company revenues
- Wall Street consensus remains "Strong Buy" on Meta (37 Buy vs. 1 Sell)
- Settlements (TikTok, Snap) suggest manageable resolution paths
- Companies can implement design changes (disable autoplay, remove infinite scroll for minors) to mitigate future claims

### Key Risks to Monitor
1. MDL bellwether trial outcomes (June and August 2026) — will set precedent for 2,400+ cases
2. New Mexico Phase 2 ruling (May 2026) — could mandate platform changes
3. EU DSA final determination and fines
4. KOSA legislative trajectory (duty of care provision)
5. Scale of mass arbitration demands against Meta
6. Whether additional states file AG lawsuits following New Mexico's success
